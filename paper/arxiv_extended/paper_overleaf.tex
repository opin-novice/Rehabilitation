\begin{abstract}
Automated rehabilitation scoring from skeleton sequences is evaluated almost exclusively on clean, frontal recordings --- a regime in which architectures are indistinguishable and deployment failures are invisible. We show both halves of that claim on KIMORE. First, clean accuracy decides nothing: a $4.91$M-parameter point-cloud transformer (PCT), a $0.66$M-parameter $SE(3)$-equivariant recurrence, and a $0.21$M-parameter GRU on hand-crafted $SO(3)$ invariants score $6.47\pm0.20$, $6.73\pm0.30$ and $6.31\pm0.16$ MAD --- every gap inside the $0.33$ MAD spread that training nondeterminism alone produces. A protocol audit shows the standard single-exercise slice of this benchmark carries \emph{no subject-level signal at all} once epoch selection is made honest, so we pool and gate every experiment on a mean-predictor floor. Second, under deployment stress the models separate completely. Our read-out is $SE(3)$-invariant \emph{by construction} --- a property of the architecture, certified numerically to $2.9\times10^{-16}$ with a flat log--log slope in rotation angle ($+0.160$) and an $8.0\times10^{8}$ fp32/fp64 ratio, and holding for arbitrary weights. Rotating the test camera therefore moves our score by $9\times10^{-6}$ MAD, while PCT decays past the mean predictor between $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. Azimuth augmentation does rescue the baseline's \emph{aggregate} accuracy, but at a flat $+1.0$ MAD cost and with a per-sequence score shift of $3.03$ MAD: a flat accuracy curve is a statistical fact about a population, not a guarantee about a patient. We report two negative results in full. Native consumption of irregular arrival times buys nothing here, and we derive why: a spectral census shows resampling destroys only $2.2\%$ of KIMORE's positional energy and acts as a denoiser, though the mechanism is real (grid-free tremor recovery holds at $r=1.000$ under $70\%$ drops where a resampled estimator decays to $0.785$). And restoring chirality --- closing a parity hole that made the invariant read-out silently $O(3)$-invariant and provably blind to trajectory handedness --- costs the viewpoint theorem nothing but buys no accuracy on a corpus of healthy-form exercises. What justifies the steerable encoder is measured, not argued: when a tracking node freezes, the hand-crafted invariant model collapses through the floor at a \emph{single} dead joint ($6.31\!\to\!9.78$ MAD) while the graph-structured encoder degrades gracefully. Among models with an exact viewpoint guarantee, only the equivariant one survives a broken sensor --- at $7.4\times$ fewer parameters than the baseline. Because the guarantee is architectural rather than learned, it is also co-designed for the edge: the read-out streams causally at $8.9$~ms per frame ($112$~fps, a score before the exercise ends), and under int8 quantisation weight-rounding preserves the viewpoint theorem \emph{exactly} while only activation-rounding perturbs the reported score --- by $0.05$~MAD, two orders below the clinical floor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automated assessment of physical rehabilitation exercises from skeleton sequences promises objective, remote, and continuous monitoring of therapy \cite{capecci2019kimore}. The dominant evaluation regime, however, is a narrow one. Models are trained and tested on clean, frontal recordings from a single sensor, and are ranked by a single aggregate error against a physician's score \cite{karlov2024,abedi2023,kuang2026,pct2026}. That regime rewards capacity and penalises nothing that a deployed system would actually encounter: a camera placed against a different wall, a tracking node that stops updating, a dropped frame.

This paper is an argument that the regime, not the architectures, is the problem --- and it begins by conceding the point the regime is supposed to settle. On clean, frontal KIMORE our proposed model \textbf{does not beat} the point-cloud transformer baseline. Neither does a GRU fed six lines of hand-crafted invariant features. The three are statistically indistinguishable, and we can say so precisely because we first measured what a difference has to exceed to be a difference: training on this corpus is not bit-reproducible (nondeterministic cuDNN and scatter-add kernels), and repeated fits of an \emph{identical} configuration differ by $\approx\!0.33$~MAD --- larger than every clean-accuracy gap between the leading models in this paper. Any single-run comparison on this benchmark is therefore reporting its own seed.

The situation is worse than a tie. Auditing the reference protocol on the single-exercise slice on which KIMORE results are most often reported, we find that the published practice of selecting the reported epoch by its test-set minimum is worth $+1.111$~MAD ($16.8\%$) --- and that once it is removed, the honest number is \emph{statistically indistinguishable from a model that ignores its input entirely} (paired subject-cluster bootstrap $\Delta=-0.081$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.803,+0.625]$). It is not subject leakage: on that slice each subject contributes one recording, so a random split is already subject-disjoint. The slice simply has no subject-level signal to measure. Pooling the five exercises restores it, and every experiment below is consequently pooled, subject-disjoint, and gated on provably clearing a per-exercise mean-predictor floor --- because a model that ignores its input has \emph{zero} degradation under any corruption and would win every robustness plot ever drawn.

With clean accuracy neutralised as a discriminator, the question becomes what a structural guarantee buys. We formulate assessment as an \emph{invariant read-out of an equivariant representation}: a steerable message-passing encoder on the skeleton graph produces per-joint features carrying $O(3)$ irreducible representations, an explicit projection onto a generating set of invariants discharges the symmetry, and a recurrence consumes the resulting invariant code together with the sensor's actual inter-arrival times. Invariance of the predicted score under any rigid motion of the camera is then a theorem about the architecture (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}), true for arbitrary weights --- trained, untrained, or adversarially chosen --- rather than a behaviour induced by augmenting the training set.

The distinction is not scholastic, and the experiments are designed to make it bite. A rotation-augmented baseline has a \emph{flat aggregate accuracy curve} across camera azimuth; by the metric the field reports, it has solved viewpoint. Its per-sequence degradation is $3.03$~MAD: individual patients' scores swing by three points of fifty as the camera moves, and the errors merely cancel in the mean. Ours move by $9\times10^{-6}$. A screening instrument is required to return the same patient the same score from a different room, and that is a statement about each prediction, not about a population average. We therefore report degradation and accuracy side by side throughout, and neither alone.

We also report, in full, the two places where the structural story does \emph{not} pay. Consuming irregular arrivals natively --- the standard motivation for continuous-time models --- confers no advantage on KIMORE; the fixed-grid baseline is better on both accuracy and stability at every drop level, under an identical hash-locked augmentation budget. Rather than excuse this, we derive it: a model-free spectral census shows $97.7\%$ of the corpus's positional energy lies below the baseline's resampling corner, so the resampling operator destroys $2.2\%$ of the signal and mostly discards differencing-amplified sensor noise. It is a \emph{denoiser}, and the null was predictable before the experiment was run. That the mechanism nevertheless exists we confirm by placing signal in the destroyed band: a physiologically calibrated $5$~Hz tremor is recovered from the true irregular stamps at $r=1.000$ under drop rates up to $70\%$, while a resampled estimator decays to $0.785$. The claim is a scope boundary, not a defeat. Likewise, closing the model's parity hole --- the invariant cut we and, to our knowledge, every comparable construction emit is composed entirely of parity-\emph{even} features, and is therefore silently invariant to a mirror of the entire performance, hence blind to trajectory handedness --- costs the viewpoint theorem nothing but earns no accuracy on a corpus of healthy-form prescribed exercises, which contains no chiral pathology to detect. We adopt the fix anyway, on the grounds that the architecture should not \emph{assume} handedness is irrelevant, and we say plainly that it is unrewarded here.

Finally, the tie with the hand-crafted invariant model leaves an obligation that elegance cannot discharge: if a plain GRU on named geometric features is exactly as accurate \emph{and} exactly as viewpoint-invariant, the steerable machinery is unjustified. We discharge it with a measurement. When Kinect tracking nodes freeze --- the characteristic failure of a consumer depth sensor --- the hand-crafted model does not degrade gracefully; it fails outright: a \emph{single} dead joint drives it from $6.31$ to $9.78$~MAD, through the mean-predictor floor, because its features \emph{name} the joints they depend on. The graph encoder pools learned messages over the skeleton and dilutes the same failure across $25$ joints. This is invisible in every clean-accuracy column in the paper, which is exactly why it had to be measured separately.

Finally, we treat the viewpoint guarantee as a \emph{systems} property, not only a mathematical one: we audit its precision budget under aggressive integer quantisation and convert the read-out to true frame-by-frame causal streaming, and find that geometric exactness survives to int8 --- where only activation-rounding, not weight-rounding, perturbs it --- and runs strictly causally at $8.9$~ms per frame with no full-clip buffering, so a structural safety guarantee and low-power, real-time operation are co-designed rather than traded off. This is the third axis on which architecture, and not accuracy, is the decisive variable.

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A protocol audit that establishes what the benchmark can measure.} Optimistic epoch selection inflates the standard KIMORE single-exercise result by $16.8\%$, and the honest number does not beat a mean predictor. We identify the mechanism (it is \emph{not} subject leakage), fix a pooled protocol, quantify the $0.33$~MAD nondeterminism floor below which no accuracy difference is interpretable, and gate every subsequent claim on clearing the floor.

\item \textbf{Exact viewpoint invariance, certified rather than asserted.} An $SE(3)$-invariant read-out over a steerable encoder, audited by eight numerical gates (E1--E8): representation orthogonality $\le10^{-12}$, violation at machine roundoff with a \emph{flat} log--log slope in rotation angle ($+0.160$), an fp32/fp64 violation ratio of $8.0\times10^{8}$, and translation invariance at $2.6\times10^{-16}$. Magnitude alone would not license the word ``zero''; flatness and precision-scaling exclude a small modelling violation masquerading as noise. Measured cross-viewpoint degradation is $9\times10^{-6}$~MAD against the baseline's $9.42$, and the baseline crosses the mean-predictor floor between $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. The certificate quantifies over Haar-random $SO(3)$ rather than a single azimuth sweep, so a real camera relocation --- such as the $45^\circ$ displacement defining NTU~RGB+D~$60$ Cross-View --- is one instance it already covers, with no retraining. On that benchmark the identical encoder, trained without rotation augmentation, generalises to the held-out camera at $82.98\%$ top-$1$.

\item \textbf{A co-designed deployment path: a structural quantisation audit and a causal streaming read-out.} The guarantee is not merely an fp64 identity, and we engineer it to hold on the hardware a home device actually runs. Auditing its precision budget from fp64 to int8, we find the theorem survives \emph{weight} quantisation exactly (equivariance holds for arbitrary weights, so a quantised model is simply a different equivariant model), and that the $\times10^{5}$ floor cliff is attributable \emph{solely} to activation grid-rounding of the steerable channels --- a structural, not a weight-precision, phenomenon --- while the reported clinical score still moves only $0.05$~MAD, below the floor. And because the invariance lives entirely in a per-frame encoder and an invariant cut, the read-out converts to a strictly causal, unidirectional form with a running-mean pool that emits a first score $8.9$~ms after the first frame ($112$~fps) and preserves invariance to $1.4\times10^{-8}$, giving the architecture an honest time-to-first-score for real-time use (Sec.~\ref{sec:deploy}). The theorem is engineered to hold from fp64 to int8 and from full-clip to first-frame, not only in exact arithmetic on a full recording.

\item \textbf{The capacity tax of learned invariance, and the sense in which a flat curve is not invariance.} Azimuth augmentation does flatten the baseline's aggregate response, at a flat $+1.0$~MAD penalty that parks it \emph{at} the floor --- and it leaves a $3.03$~MAD per-sequence score shift that aggregate MAD cannot express. We grant the baseline this remedy explicitly rather than defeat a strawman.

\item \textbf{A parity hole in invariant skeleton read-outs, exhibited and closed.} Gate E6 shows the parity-even cut returns \emph{bit-identical} output on a mirrored body: an unintended $O(3)$-invariance. We restore $SO(3)$ with pseudo-scalar triple products and anatomical signed volumes, re-certify read-out invariance at $2.9\times10^{-16}$ (the fix is free), and separately measure --- against the natural objection --- that reflection-blindness was never left/right-blindness (gate E7).

\item \textbf{Two negative results, one of them derived.} Grid-free consumption of arrival times buys nothing on KIMORE, and a spectral census predicts exactly that ($2.2\%$ of positional energy destroyed; the mechanism verified live at $r=1.000$ tremor recovery). Chirality is restored but unrewarded. We state both as scope boundaries with the corpus properties that would reward them.

\item \textbf{A measured justification for the steerable encoder: sensor-node failure.} Among models carrying an exact viewpoint guarantee, only the graph-structured one survives a broken tracking node ($+3.76$ vs.\ $+12.05$~MAD over $8$ dead joints). We also record, against ourselves, that the point-cloud transformer is the most node-robust model of the three --- robustness to node failure is not a property of equivariance and we do not claim it is. The defensible claim is the Pareto one: our model is the only arm that clears the floor under \emph{both} a $90^\circ$ camera move and two dead joints while retaining a per-prediction guarantee, at $7.4\times$ fewer parameters than the baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Rehabilitation Exercise Assessment from Skeletons}
KIMORE \cite{capecci2019kimore} is the standard benchmark: subjects perform five trunk and hip exercises, each scored $0$--$50$ by a clinician. Reported results span a wide range of architectures --- ST-GCN variants with supervised contrastive objectives \cite{karlov2024}, cross-modal augmentation \cite{abedi2023}, dual-stream spatio-temporal graph networks \cite{kuang2026}, and, most relevantly here, point-cloud transformers that treat the skeleton as an unordered set of $3$D points with axial self-attention \cite{pct2026}. Ismail-Fawaz et al.\ \cite{rehabpile2026} aggregate KIMORE, UI-PRMD \cite{vakanski2018uiprmd} and IRDS under cross-subject splits and report error rather than rank correlation, and their protocol discipline is the closest antecedent of our own audit.

Two properties of this literature motivate the present work. First, evaluation is uniformly in-distribution: same sensor, same camera placement, no simulated hardware failure. Second, protocols differ enough that headline numbers are not comparable --- a point we make quantitative in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:protocol}, where we isolate optimistic epoch selection as a $16.8\%$ effect on the most commonly reported slice, and where we find that slice to carry no subject-level signal at all. To our knowledge no prior work on this task reports a mean-predictor floor, and without one a degradation curve cannot be interpreted.

\subsection{Equivariant and Invariant Architectures}
Group-equivariant networks impose symmetry as a constraint on the hypothesis class rather than a target of fitting \cite{cohen2016gconv}. For $3$D geometry the steerable family --- tensor-field networks \cite{thomas2018tfn}, $SE(3)$-Transformers \cite{fuchs2020se3transformer}, and the \texttt{e3nn} framework \cite{geiger2022e3nn} --- carries features in irreducible representations of $O(3)$ and constrains every layer to intertwine with the Wigner-$D$ action, guaranteeing equivariance for arbitrary weights. Lighter constructions obtain $E(n)$-equivariance without spherical harmonics \cite{satorras2021egnn}, or lift a network to $SO(3)$-equivariance by replacing scalar neurons with vector-valued ones \cite{deng2021vectorneurons}. We build the spatial encoder on \cite{geiger2022e3nn}, but the paper's claim is deliberately \emph{not} about steerable machinery: we show a GRU on hand-crafted $SO(3)$ invariants attains the same accuracy and the same exact viewpoint guarantee at a third of the parameters, and we then isolate --- by measurement, in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:nodefail} --- the one axis on which the two genuinely differ.

An orthogonal line makes a non-equivariant backbone invariant by canonicalisation or frame averaging \cite{puny2022frameaveraging}, which is exact but pays a forward-pass or a learned-canonicaliser cost. The two routes differ in where exactness comes from. A canonicaliser aligns each input to an estimated reference frame; the invariance is then exact \emph{only in so far as the frame estimate is}, and a learned or ill-conditioned frame (e.g.\ under a near-degenerate pose or a symmetric skeleton) reintroduces the very per-prediction variance we set out to eliminate, while frame averaging over a rotation group is exact but multiplies the forward cost. Our invariant cut estimates no frame at all: it is an explicit projection onto a generating set of invariants of the latent, exact for arbitrary weights by Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}, and Sec.~\ref{sec:method:cut} makes the resulting group precise --- including the observation, which we have not seen stated, that the natural such cut is complete for $O(3)$ rather than $SO(3)$ and is therefore blind to trajectory handedness until pseudo-scalars are admitted. A head-to-head comparison against a canonicalisation front-end --- on both aggregate accuracy and the per-sequence degradation of Sec.~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint}, where a frame-estimation error would surface --- is a natural extension we do not run here.

\subsection{Learned Invariance and Its Price}
The default remedy for viewpoint variation is training-time augmentation, and it works in the sense the field measures: an augmented model's aggregate error becomes approximately flat in camera angle. We grant the baseline exactly this remedy and report what it costs --- a flat accuracy penalty, and a per-sequence score shift that survives the augmentation because augmentation makes errors \emph{cancel} rather than \emph{vanish}. The distinction between a symmetry that holds in expectation over a training distribution and one that holds identically for every input is, in a clinical instrument, the whole point; it is expressible only if degradation is reported per sequence, which is why we report it alongside accuracy everywhere.

\subsection{Irregular Sampling and Continuous-Time Models}
Depth-sensor streams are not uniformly sampled: our anchor KIMORE recording has $C_V=0.25$ in its inter-arrival times with genuine multi-frame dropouts. The standard treatment is to resample onto a uniform grid; the standard alternative is a continuous-time model that consumes arrival times natively --- Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neuralode}, latent ODEs and ODE-RNNs for irregularly sampled series \cite{rubanova2019odernn}, Neural CDEs driven by an interpolant of the observations \cite{kidger2020neuralcde}, or recurrences with explicit decay between observations \cite{che2018grud}. We implement the Neural CDE variant, certify it to the same standard as the proposed model, and report it as a \emph{control}: it satisfies an exact equivariance certificate and \emph{fails} the mean-predictor floor, which is the cleanest demonstration we can offer that a structural guarantee is worthless without measured signal, and the reason our evaluation gates on the floor at all.

Our analysis of the adaptive solver (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:cde-control}) identifies a failure mode we have not seen documented: a Dormand--Prince controller \cite{dormand1980prince} accepts or rejects steps on a scalar reduction of the embedded error, and the standard per-component scaled RMS norm is \emph{not} group-invariant even when the vector field is exactly equivariant and the representation orthogonal. The rotated and unrotated integrations then receive different step grids and silently de-align. We prove the condition under which this happens and construct a per-irrep-normalised norm that removes it, driving grid divergence to identically zero.

Finally, the negative result of Sec.~\ref{sec:results:resampling} is stated as a bandwidth condition and verified in both directions, the constructive half using the classical fact that irregularly sampled data have no single Nyquist frequency and do not alias --- the basis of the Lomb--Scargle construction \cite{vanderplas2018lombscargle} --- which is precisely why a grid-free estimator recovers a $5$~Hz tremor that a uniform grid at the same average rate folds onto itself irrecoverably.

\subsection{Robustness to Missing and Corrupted Joints}
Occlusion and tracking failure are standard concerns in skeleton-based action recognition, typically addressed by masking-based pretraining or by attention that can down-weight anomalous joints \cite{yan2018stgcn}. We adopt the failure literally --- a Kinect node that stops updating and reports a stale estimate --- and use it as the discriminating experiment between two models that are otherwise indistinguishable in accuracy \emph{and} in viewpoint guarantee. The result is a mechanism, not a leaderboard entry: features that name their joints are brittle to a dead joint in a way that pooled graph messages are not.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

We formulate exercise assessment as an \emph{invariant read-out of an equivariant representation of
an irregularly sampled pose sequence}. Two structural properties hold by construction rather than by
fitting:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Exact viewpoint invariance.} The predicted score is provably unchanged under any
rigid motion of the camera. This is a theorem about the architecture
(\S\ref{sec:method:cut}--\S\ref{sec:method:cert}), certified numerically to machine roundoff, and it
is the paper's central claim.
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Grid-free consumption of arrival times.} The model reads the sensor's actual
inter-arrival intervals $\Delta t_k$ and never assumes a uniform frame axis, so no resampling
operator is applied to its input. We are careful to state what this does \emph{not} buy: on KIMORE it
confers no accuracy or stability advantage over a fixed-grid baseline
(\S\ref{sec:results:resampling}). It is an architectural property that we characterise, not a
performance claim that we assert.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, $J$ is the number of skeletal joints, $g=(R,\bm{t})\in SE(3)$ acts on joint observations
$\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times J}$ by $g\cdot\bm{x}=R\bm{x}+\bm{t}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}$, and
$\{t_k\}_{k=0}^{L-1}$ are the sensor's own, non-uniform arrival stamps.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Irregular Arrivals Without a Frame Grid}
\label{sec:method:irregular}

A Kinect stream delivers observations at authentic, non-uniform times recorded by the sensor clock
(the \texttt{RelativeTime} field; our anchor recording has coefficient of variation
$C_V=\sigma_{\Delta t}/\mu_{\Delta t}=0.25$ with genuine multi-frame dropouts). We first remove global
body placement by referring every joint to the root,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:root}
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_k}=\bm{x}_{t_k}-\bm{x}^{\text{root}}_{t_k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times J},
\end{equation}
%
which makes translation invariance exact and discharges it \emph{upstream of the network}. We are
explicit that the encoder of \S\ref{sec:method:encoder} is \emph{not} itself translation-invariant --
it reads joint radii $\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_j\|$ and seeds vector channels from $\tilde{\bm{x}}_j$ -- so
the claim is true only with respect to the root, which is exactly where
\eqref{eq:root} establishes it and where our certificate (E5) tests it, on raw world coordinates.

The temporal geometry enters through the inter-arrival intervals themselves,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dt}
\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1},\qquad \Delta t_0 \equiv 0 ,
\end{equation}
%
which are supplied to the recurrence of \S\ref{sec:method:recurrence} as an input channel. There is no
frame axis to align and no uniform grid to assume: a fixed-grid architecture must first apply a
resampling operator $\mathcal{R}$ (\S\ref{sec:method:spectral}), whereas here
$\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{Id}$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The $SO(3)$-Equivariant Spatial Encoder}
\label{sec:method:encoder}

Each frame is encoded independently by a shared, steerable message-passing network on the skeleton
graph, whose edges $\mathcal{E}$ are the anatomical bones. Per joint, the latent carries a direct sum
of $O(3)$ irreducible representations --- $n_0$ Type-0 (even scalar, $0e$) and $n_1$ Type-1 (odd
vector, $1o$) channels,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:irreps}
\begin{gathered}
h_j \in n_0\!\cdot\!(0e)\,\oplus\, n_1\!\cdot\!(1o),
\qquad h_j=\big(s_j,\,v_j\big),\\[2pt]
s_j\in\mathbb{R}^{n_0},\quad v_j\in\mathbb{R}^{n_1\times3},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
on which the group acts by the block-diagonal Wigner-$D$ representation
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho}
\rho(g)=I_{n_0}\;\oplus\;\big(I_{n_1}\otimes D_1(g)\big),\qquad D_1(g)\in SO(3),
\end{equation}
%
in the real spherical-harmonic basis. Every block is \emph{orthogonal},
$D_{\ell}(g)^{\!\top}D_{\ell}(g)=I$, which we certify to
$\max_g\|\rho(g)^{\!\top}\rho(g)-I\|_F\le10^{-12}$ in double precision.

\paragraph{Initialisation.} Scalars are seeded from invariants (joint radius and a learned joint
embedding $\bm{e}_j$), and vectors from the joint's own position scaled by learned \emph{scalars}
--- a scalar times a $1o$ feature is again $1o$, hence equivariant:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:init}
s_j^{(0)}=\mathrm{MLP}\big(\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_j\|,\;\bm{e}_j\big),
\qquad
v_j^{(0)}=\bm{\gamma}\otimes\tilde{\bm{x}}_j,\quad \bm{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Steerable message passing.} Geometry enters only through the relative bone vector
$\bm{r}_{ij}=\tilde{\bm{x}}_i-\tilde{\bm{x}}_j$ (a $1o$ quantity), its length $d_{ij}=\|\bm{r}_{ij}\|$
(an invariant), and the spherical harmonics $Y_{ij}=Y^{\ell\le\ell_{\max}}(\bm{r}_{ij}/d_{ij})$. Edge
weights are produced by a radial network of \emph{invariants} only,
$w_{ij}=\varphi\big(\mathrm{RBF}(d_{ij}),\,\bm{b}_{ij}\big)$ with $\bm{b}_{ij}$ a learned bone-type
embedding, so the weights themselves are group-invariant. Messages are formed by a
Clebsch--Gordan-constrained tensor product and aggregated:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mp}
\begin{gathered}
\bm{m}_{ij}=\mathcal{T}_{w_{ij}}\big(h_j,\;Y_{ij}\big),\\[2pt]
h_i \;\leftarrow\; h_i + W\!\left[\Gamma\Big(\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\bm{m}_{ij}\Big)\right].
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
The tensor product is supported only on admissible paths
$|\ell_1-\ell_2|\le\ell_{\text{out}}\le\ell_1+\ell_2$ with $p_1p_2=p_{\text{out}}$, and the
Wigner--Eckart theorem then guarantees, for \emph{every} admissible weight and \emph{every} $g$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tp-intertwine}
\mathcal{T}_{w}\big(\rho(g)h_j,\;D(g)Y_{ij}\big)=\rho(g)\,\mathcal{T}_{w}(h_j,Y_{ij}).
\end{equation}
%
The gate $\Gamma$ is an equivariant nonlinearity: $\tanh$ acts on feature scalars while
sigmoid-activated \emph{gate} scalars multiply the $1o$ channels; since a scalar commutes with
$D_1(g)$, $\Gamma$ preserves \eqref{eq:tp-intertwine}, as does the CG-constrained linear map $W$.
Equivariance therefore holds for arbitrary weights --- it is a property of the construction, not of
training.

\begin{proposition}[Elimination of non-scalar biases]
\label{prop:bias}
A learnable additive bias $b$ on any $1o$ channel breaks equivariance unless $b=\bm{0}$; biases are
admissible only on $0e$ channels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under $g$ a $1o$ feature transforms as $v\mapsto D_1(g)v$, while a constant bias transforms trivially.
Equivariance of $v\mapsto v+b$ requires $D_1(g)v+b=D_1(g)(v+b)$ for all $g$, i.e.\ $D_1(g)b=b$ for all
$g\in SO(3)$; the only vector fixed by the full rotation group is $b=\bm{0}$. For $0e$ channels
$D_0(g)=1$ and the constraint is vacuous.
\end{proof}

Accordingly every steerable linear layer is instantiated with \texttt{biases=False}, and we verify
structurally that no bias parameter exists on any non-scalar output.

\subsection{The Invariant Cut: Where the Theorem Is Discharged}
\label{sec:method:cut}

The encoder is \emph{equivariant}; the score must be \emph{invariant}. The transition is made by an
explicit projection $\Pi$ onto a generating set of invariants of the latent \eqref{eq:irreps}. The
\emph{parity-even} generators are the scalars, the norms of the vector channels, and their pairwise
angles. We use unit-normalised inner products (cosines) and $\log(1+\cdot)$ magnitudes rather than raw
dot products, which are quadratic in feature scale and numerically destabilise the read-out at no
informational gain.

Those generators alone, however, are complete for the \emph{full} orthogonal group $O(3)$, not for the
$SO(3)$ we require --- every one of them is fixed by a reflection. A cut built from them is therefore
invariant to a global mirror of the performance, and consequently blind to \emph{trajectory
handedness}: a clockwise and an anticlockwise limb circle have identical bone lengths, identical
pairwise distances and identical speeds at every instant, and no parity-even feature can separate
them. Any pathology whose signature is the \emph{sense} of a rotation --- compensatory trunk torsion,
a circumducting gait, the direction of a scapular hitch --- lies entirely in the subspace such a cut
deletes. We therefore complete the cut with the \emph{parity-odd} generators, the pseudo-scalar triple
products, taken over unit vectors so that they are bounded in $[-1,1]$ exactly as the cosines are:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cut}
\begin{aligned}
\pi_j=\Big[\;
&\underbrace{\tanh s_j \;\big\|\; \log\big(1+\|v_j^{c}\|\big)_{c} \;\big\|\;
\big\langle \hat v_j^{\,c},\,\hat v_j^{\,c'}\big\rangle_{c<c'}}_{\text{parity-even}\;(0e)}
\;\big\|\;\\[-2pt]
&\underbrace{\det\big[\hat v_j^{\,a},\hat v_j^{\,b},\hat v_j^{\,c}\big]_{a<b<c}}_{\text{parity-odd}\;(0o)}
\;\Big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
with $\hat v = v/\|v\|$. These are pooled over joints by concatenated mean and max and concatenated
with the invariant bone lengths $d_{ij}=\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}-\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}\|$ and with a fixed set
$\mathcal{T}$ of \emph{anatomical} signed volumes $\chi_\tau$ --- normalised determinants over
physically meaningful joint triads and kinematic chains (the shoulder--shoulder--head frame, the
pelvis, the signed volume of three consecutive bones along each limb). The latter do not rely on the
encoder learning to construct chirality for itself:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pi}
\begin{gathered}
\Pi(h,\tilde{\bm{x}})=\Big[\;\mathrm{mean}_j\,\pi_j \;\big\|\; \max_j\,\pi_j \;\big\|\\[2pt]
\big(d_{ij}\big)_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\;\big\|\;\big(\chi_\tau\big)_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\;\Big].
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Structural specification of the invariant sets.} Table~\ref{tab:invsets} states the two
sets explicitly --- their generators, $O(3)$ irrep types, dimensions, and pooling --- for the
deployed channel widths ($n_0=32$ type-$0$, $n_1=8$ type-$1$). One point is worth making precise
because it is easy to mis-specify: the parity-even and parity-odd per-joint generators are pooled by
the \emph{same} permutation-invariant $\mathrm{mean}\oplus\max$ over joints; there is no separate
``signed'' pooling operator for the chiral channels, and none is needed, because a mean or max of
bounded signed pseudo-scalars is itself a valid invariant. The per-bone lengths and the per-frame
anatomical volumes are appended \emph{unpooled}, in fixed index order (this index-preservation is what
Remark~\ref{rem:laterality} relies on). Under the masked variant, a liveness gate zeroes any generator
--- pooled feature, bone length, or signed volume --- that touches a failed joint, which is what makes
the dead-node invariance of Sec.~\ref{sec:results:nodefail} exact rather than approximate. The
parity-even subtotal is exactly the $160$-dimensional projection whose precision budget is audited in
Sec.~\ref{sec:deploy:precision}; the parity-odd completion adds $123$ dimensions and narrows the
guaranteed group from $O(3)$ to $SO(3)$ at no cost to the viewpoint theorem
(Remark~\ref{rem:parity}).

\paragraph{Numerical stability of the pseudo-scalars.} The parity-odd entries divide by a vector
norm, and a determinant is discontinuous in the sign of a vanishing argument, so the unit
normalisation $\hat v=v/\|v\|$ is regularised by a fixed floor,
$\hat v=v/(\|v\|+\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, applied identically to the learned $1o$
channels and to the anatomical difference vectors. We do not clamp or skip terms; the floor is the
only intervention. It is inert except when $\|v\|\lesssim\varepsilon$ --- a numerically null channel
or a collapsed anatomical triad --- because the determinant is taken over \emph{unit} vectors and is
therefore bounded in $[-1,1]$ regardless of $\varepsilon$, so the read-out cannot be inflated by a
small denominator. The one regime that genuinely produces a near-zero norm is a failed tracking node
that freezes two joints onto each other; there the anatomical volume is not merely small but
sign-meaningless, and we zero it explicitly with a per-frame liveness mask (a chiral frame spanning a
dead joint contributes exactly $0$ rather than an ill-conditioned $\pm1$) rather than let the floor
decide its sign. Because the pseudo-scalars are bounded and the floor only regularises a vanishing
denominator, the deployed read-out is insensitive to $\varepsilon$ over many decades: the invariance
gate E8 below ($2.9\times10^{-16}$) and every accuracy number in Section~\ref{sec:results} are computed
with this floor active.

\begin{proposition}[$SO(3)$-invariance of the read-out]
\label{prop:invariance}
$\Pi\big(\rho(g)h,\;R\tilde{\bm{x}}\big)=\Pi\big(h,\tilde{\bm{x}}\big)$ for every proper rotation
$g\in SO(3)$. Consequently every downstream computation --- and hence the predicted score --- is
invariant, \emph{for arbitrary weights}. Under an improper $g$ the parity-odd entries change sign, so
the read-out is \emph{not} invariant to reflections.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Scalars carry $D_0(g)=1$ and are fixed. Vector channels transform as $v^c\mapsto D_1(g)v^c$ with
$D_1(g)$ orthogonal, so $\|v^c\|$ is fixed and
$\langle D_1 \hat v^{c}, D_1 \hat v^{c'}\rangle=\langle \hat v^{c},\hat v^{c'}\rangle$. Bone lengths
are norms of differences of positions and are fixed by any rigid motion. For the triple products,
$\det[D_1\hat v^{a},D_1\hat v^{b},D_1\hat v^{c}]=\det(D_1)\,\det[\hat v^{a},\hat v^{b},\hat v^{c}]$,
and $\det D_1(g)=+1$ precisely when $g$ is proper; the same identity gives the sign flip for improper
$g$. Mean and max are permutation-symmetric functions of invariants, hence invariant.
\end{proof}

Two consequences deserve emphasis, because both are commonly mis-stated.

\begin{remark}[Restoring chirality costs the viewpoint theorem nothing]
\label{rem:parity}
A camera relocation induces a \emph{proper} rotation, for which $\det D_1(g)=+1$. The pseudo-scalars
are therefore exactly invariant under every transformation the viewpoint claim quantifies over, and
narrowing the guaranteed group from $O(3)$ to $SO(3)$ forfeits none of it. This is not an argument but
a measurement: gate E8 of Section~\ref{sec:method:cert} certifies read-out invariance at
$2.9\times10^{-16}$ \emph{after} the parity-odd channels are admitted, and the measured cross-viewpoint
degradation is unchanged at machine precision (Section~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint}). The price is
$16.7\%$ more parameters and, on KIMORE, no measurable change in accuracy --- the parity-odd subspace
carries no score-relevant signal in a corpus of healthy-form exercises. We report that null rather than
suppress it. The point is that the architecture no longer \emph{assumes} it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Reflection-blindness is not left/right-blindness]
\label{rem:laterality}
It is tempting to read $O(3)$-invariance as an inability to distinguish a movement performed with the
left limb from the same movement performed with the right --- which, for unilateral pathology, would be
disqualifying. It does not follow, and it is false. Joint \emph{indices} are preserved throughout the
pipeline: the embedding of \eqref{eq:init} is keyed by joint identity, bone lengths are emitted per
bone in a fixed order, and the speed channels of \eqref{eq:speed} are indexed per joint and never
pooled. A left-arm raise and a right-arm raise are not related by a reflection; they are related by a
reflection \emph{composed with} the left/right relabelling of joint indices, and no model here is
invariant to that relabelling. Gate E7 measures it directly: swapping the left and right joint indices
moves the score by $4.5\times10^{-2}$ (parity-even cut) and $6.2\times10^{-2}$ (full cut), while a pure
reflection moves the parity-even score by exactly $0$. The defect the pseudo-scalars repair is
handedness of the \emph{trajectory}, not identity of the \emph{limb}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Temporal Recurrence on Actual Inter-Arrival Times}
\label{sec:method:recurrence}

The encoder is a purely \emph{spatial}, static map: it describes each frame's pose but never its
motion. We therefore hand the recurrence two families of explicitly invariant motion channels,
computed from the actual stamps. For joint $j$ at arrival $k$, the displacement and the true velocity
magnitude are
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:speed}
\delta_{k,j}=\big\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_j(t_k)-\tilde{\bm{x}}_j(t_{k-1})\big\|,
\qquad
\sigma_{k,j}=\frac{\delta_{k,j}}{\max(\Delta t_k,\;\epsilon)} .
\end{equation}
%
Both are norms of differences of positions, hence $SO(3)$-invariant by construction: they cost the
theorem nothing, and the certificate (E2) re-verifies this rather than assuming it. Dividing by the
\emph{actual} $\Delta t_k$ --- not by a nominal frame period --- is what makes $\sigma$ a
velocity rather than a per-frame increment, and it is the point at which irregular sampling enters
the model as information instead of as a defect to be interpolated away. We include these channels
because a recurrence trained on 45 subjects does not reliably learn to difference its own inputs; the
ablation without them is reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

The per-arrival input is the concatenation of the invariant pose code, the interval, the motion
channels, and a one-hot exercise identifier:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:input}
u_k=\Big[\;\Pi_k \;\big\|\; \Delta t_k \;\big\|\; \log(1+\delta_k) \;\big\|\; \log(1+\sigma_k)
\;\big\|\; \mathrm{onehot}(e)\;\Big],
\end{equation}
%
which a bidirectional GRU consumes in arrival order. The sequence is reduced by a length-masked mean
over the $n$ genuine arrivals (padding excluded), and an MLP head emits the score:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:head}
\hat y=\psi\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k<n}\mathrm{GRU}(u)_k\right).
\end{equation}
%
Since every component of $u_k$ is invariant by Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} and
\eqref{eq:speed}, and $\psi$ acts on invariants, the composite map
$\tilde{\bm{x}}\mapsto\hat y$ is exactly $SE(3)$-invariant --- for any weights, trained or not. The
recurrence is discrete and consumes no solver; there is no integrator, no step-size controller, and
therefore none of the adaptive-solver failure modes analysed in \S\ref{sec:method:cde-control}.

\subsection{Numerical Certificate}
\label{sec:method:cert}

An architectural symmetry argument is worth only as much as its numerical audit, because a symmetry
can be silently broken by an implementation detail --- and, as E6 shows, an \emph{unintended} symmetry
can be silently acquired. We therefore certify the \emph{deployed} model
(E1--E8, \texttt{src/certify\_egru.py}). Gates E1--E5 establish that the invariance we claim is
present; E6--E8 establish that the invariance we claim is the one we actually have.

\begin{itemize}
\item[E1] \textbf{Representation orthogonality:}
$\max_g\|\rho(g)^{\!\top}\rho(g)-I\|_F\le10^{-12}$ (fp64).
\item[E2] \textbf{Invariance of the motion channels:} $\delta,\sigma$ verified invariant, not assumed.
\item[E3] \textbf{Rotation sweep:} the score violation is $\sim\!10^{-16}$ across
$\theta\in[0.05,3.0]$~rad, and --- decisively --- its log--log slope against $\theta$ is $+0.160$,
i.e.\ \emph{flat}. A modelling violation grows with angle; roundoff does not.
\item[E4] \textbf{Precision scaling:} repeating the identical computation in single and double
precision gives
$r=\bar\delta^{\,\mathrm{fp32}}/\bar\delta^{\,\mathrm{fp64}}
=2.37\times10^{-7}/2.96\times10^{-16}=8.0\times10^{8}$,
an eight-order drop tracking the ratio of machine epsilons. A genuine symmetry violation would be
precision-\emph{independent} ($r\approx1$).
\item[E5] \textbf{Translation invariance of the pipeline:} tested on raw world coordinates,
$|s(\bm{x}+\bm{c})-s(\bm{x})|/|s(\bm{x})|=2.6\times10^{-16}$.
\item[E6] \textbf{Parity (the group is $SO(3)$, not $O(3)$):} under a reflection $M$, the
parity-even cut gives $|s(M\bm{x})-s(\bm{x})|/|s(\bm{x})|=\mathbf{0}$ \emph{exactly} --- bit-identical
output on a mirrored body, an unintended $O(3)$-invariance --- whereas the completed cut of
\eqref{eq:cut} gives $3.1\times10^{-2}$. Because the model is $SO(3)$-invariant, all improper
transformations are equivalent up to a rotation, so a single reflection certifies the entire coset.
\item[E7] \textbf{Laterality:} permuting the left and right joint \emph{indices} (a relabelling, not a
reflection) moves the score by $4.5\times10^{-2}$ and $6.2\times10^{-2}$ for the two cuts
respectively. Both models separate a left-limb from a right-limb movement
(Remark~\ref{rem:laterality}).
\item[E8] \textbf{The fix is free:} read-out invariance under Haar-random \emph{proper} rotations
--- arbitrary $g\in SO(3)$ drawn uniformly with rotation angle up to $\pi$, not a gravity-axis
azimuth sweep --- re-measured with the parity-odd channels active, is $2.9\times10^{-16}$ ---
unchanged. Restoring chirality costs the viewpoint theorem nothing. Gates E1--E5 are likewise
evaluated over Haar-random $SO(3)$, so the certified invariance is to the \emph{full} rotation group,
of which a camera azimuth or a cross-view camera displacement is one instance.
\end{itemize}
%
The three conditions of E3--E4 together --- violation at roundoff, \emph{flat} in $\theta$, and
$r\gg1$ --- are what upgrade ``small'' to ``zero''. Magnitude alone would not: only the flatness and
the precision scaling exclude a small modelling violation masquerading as noise. E6 is included as a
matter of method: an architecture should be audited for the symmetries it possesses, not only for the
ones it advertises, and a gate that merely confirmed our intended invariance would have passed a model
that was invariant to twice the group we wanted.

\subsection{Control: The Continuous-Flow (Neural CDE) Variant}
\label{sec:method:cde-control}

The natural continuous-time generalisation of \S\ref{sec:method:recurrence} replaces the discrete
recurrence with a controlled differential equation driven by a spline through the arrivals. We
implement it, certify it, and report it as a \textbf{control} --- not as the proposed method --- for a
reason given empirically in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:limits}: it satisfies an equally exact equivariance
certificate yet \emph{fails} the mean-predictor floor, and is therefore the cleanest available
demonstration that a structural guarantee is worthless without measured signal. Its role here is to
delimit the optimisation boundary of rigidly constrained continuous flows, not to carry a claim.

\paragraph{Control path.} The root-relative observations are interpolated by a natural cubic spline
$X:[t_0,t_{L-1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{3J}$, whose second-derivative moments $M_i=X''(t_i)$ solve, with
$h_i=t_{i+1}-t_i$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spline-moments}
\begin{gathered}
h_{i-1}M_{i-1}+2(h_{i-1}+h_i)M_i+h_iM_{i+1}\\
=6\!\left(\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_{i+1}}-\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_i}}{h_i}
-\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_i}-\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_{i-1}}}{h_{i-1}}\right),
\quad M_0=M_{L-1}=\bm{0}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
The spline is \emph{linear} in its control points and \emph{reproduces constants}, so it commutes with
the group action, $\mathcal{S}[g\cdot\tilde{\bm{x}}]=g\cdot\mathcal{S}[\tilde{\bm{x}}]$, and is defined
for arbitrary spacing $h_i$. The latent then evolves as
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cde}
\begin{gathered}
Z(s)=Z(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{s}f_\theta\big(Z(\tau),U(\tau)\big)\,d\tau,\\[2pt]
U(s)=\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{d}{ds}X_j(s)\in\mathbb{R}^3,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
with $f_\theta$ a steerable field built from the same CG-constrained tensor products and gates as
\eqref{eq:mp}, and an invariant terminal read-out $\hat y=h_\psi(Z(t_{L-1}))$. For a fixed-step
explicit integrator, $Z_{g\cdot X}(s)=\rho(g)Z_X(s)$ holds stage-by-stage to roundoff, independent of
step size; we measure $\delta_{\text{eq}}\le10^{-13}$ on the real steerable architecture.

\paragraph{The adaptive-solver failure mode, and the invariant norm.} Fixed-step exactness is
necessary but not sufficient, because a production integrator is \emph{adaptive}. A Dormand--Prince
step accepts or rejects on a scalar reduction of the embedded local error, and the accepted step
sequence --- hence the trajectory --- is a deterministic function of that scalar. If the scalar is not
group-invariant, the rotated and unrotated integrations receive \emph{different} step grids and
silently de-align. This survives an orthogonal representation, which is what makes it subtle.

\begin{proposition}[Non-invariance of the per-component norm]
\label{prop:fail}
Let $\rho(g)$ be orthogonal and the field intertwining, so $e\mapsto\rho(g)e$. The standard
component-weighted RMS norm $\mathcal{N}_W(e)=n^{-1/2}\|We\|_2$, $W=\mathrm{diag}(w_i)$ with
$w_i=(\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\cdot\max(|y^0_i|,|y^1_i|))^{-1}$, is invariant for all $e$ if and
only if $[\rho(g),W^2]=0$. For $\mathrm{rtol}>0$ this fails.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal{N}_W(\rho(g)e)^2=n^{-1}e^{\!\top}\rho(g)^{\!\top}W^2\rho(g)e$, so equality for all $e$ is
$\rho(g)^{\!\top}W^2\rho(g)=W^2$, i.e.\ $[\rho(g),W^2]=0$ by orthogonality. Within a $1o$ block,
$D_1(g)$ genuinely rotates the three components while their weights $w_a,w_b,w_c$ are generically
distinct; $[D_1(g),\mathrm{diag}(w_a,w_b,w_c)]=0$ for all $g$ forces $w_a=w_b=w_c$, which
per-component weighting violates whenever the components differ in scale.
\end{proof}
%
Empirically this is exactly what we observe under the default norm: the accepted grids diverge
($D_{\text{grid}}\approx40\text{--}90$ knots) and the drift degrades to $\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})$
\emph{despite an exactly equivariant field}. We restore invariance by making both the tolerance and
the reduction respect the irrep block structure. With $F$ irrep features of dimension
$d_f=2\ell_f+1$, set a block-constant tolerance from the \emph{invariant} block norm and reduce
isotropically:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:neq}
\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{denom}_f=\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\cdot\max\big(\|y^0_f\|_2,\|y^1_f\|_2\big),\\[2pt]
\boxed{\;\mathcal{N}_{\text{eq}}(\hat e)=\sqrt{\frac1F\sum_{f=1}^{F}\frac{1}{d_f}\|\hat e_f\|_2^2}\;}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
with $\hat e_f=e_f/\mathrm{denom}_f$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact invariance and vanishing grid divergence]
\label{prop:neq}
$\mathcal{N}_{\text{eq}}$ is invariant; consequently the adaptive controller makes identical
accept/reject decisions for $X$ and $g\cdot X$, and $D_{\text{grid}}\equiv0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each block transforms as $e_f\mapsto D_{\ell_f}(g)e_f$ with $D_{\ell_f}(g)$ orthogonal, so
$\|y^k_f\|_2$ --- and hence $\mathrm{denom}_f$ --- is invariant, and
$\|D_{\ell_f}(g)\hat e_f\|_2=\|\hat e_f\|_2$. Thus \eqref{eq:neq} is a $g$-independent weighted sum of
invariants. Equivalently the block-constant tolerance makes $W_f\propto I_{d_f}$, so
$[D_{\ell_f}(g),W_f^2]=0$ and the condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:fail} holds exactly. The reduced
error scalar therefore coincides for $X$ and $g\cdot X$, the step-size updates coincide, and the
accepted grids satisfy $\mathcal{T}(g\cdot X)=\mathcal{T}(X)$.
\end{proof}
%
Under $\mathcal{N}_{\text{eq}}$ the measured grid divergence is identically zero, the residual drift
collapses to $\approx1.2\times10^{-15}$ (flat in rotation magnitude), and the precision-scaling ratio
is $r\approx5\times10^{8}$. The continuous-flow control is thus \emph{certified to the same standard
as the proposed model} --- which is precisely what makes its failure against the floor
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results:limits}) methodologically instructive rather than merely disappointing.

\subsection{Spectral Fidelity Calibration: The Metric $\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}$}
\label{sec:method:spectral}

A fixed-grid architecture cannot consume $(t_k,\tilde{\bm{x}}_{t_k})$ directly; it must first apply a
resampling operator $\mathcal{R}$ onto a uniform grid. $\mathcal{R}$ is a low-pass operation, and we
quantify the high-frequency energy it removes with a \emph{spectral fidelity calibration metric},
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iloss}
\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}(\mathcal{R})
=\big\|\widehat{\mathcal{R}\tilde{\bm{x}}}(\omega)-\widehat{\tilde{\bm{x}}}(\omega)\big\|^2_{L^2(\omega>\omega_c)} ,
\end{equation}
%
evaluated above a cutoff $\omega_c$ in the tremor/jerk band. Calibrated on the real anchor with a
physiologically grounded $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ tremor injected at the distal joints, a
$30\%$ burst-drop regime already forces $\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}=39\%$ under linear interpolation
and $53\%$ under cubic, rising monotonically to $81\%$ at a $70\%$ drop rate. Our formulation sets
$\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{Id}$ and hence $\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}\equiv0$ by construction.

\paragraph{What this metric does and does not license.} $\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}$ is a statement
about \emph{signal}, not about \emph{prediction}. It certifies that resampling provably removes a
specific band of spectral energy; it does \emph{not} certify that the removed band is
\emph{predictive} of the clinical score. We introduce it as a calibration instrument precisely so that
this distinction can be tested rather than assumed --- and in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:resampling} the
test comes back negative: on KIMORE, where rehabilitation motion is slow and smooth relative to the
sampling rate, a fixed-grid baseline suffers a large $\mathcal{I}_{\text{loss}}$ and yet loses
essentially no accuracy, because the discarded band carries little task-relevant information. Indeed
$\mathcal{R}$ acts there as an effective \emph{denoiser}. We therefore state plainly what the metric
establishes --- the operational envelope within which grid-free modelling could matter --- and we
decline to infer a downstream penalty from a spectral one, as an earlier version of this work did.

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:method:protocol}

\paragraph{Corruption model anchored to real kinematics.}
The base signal is a real Kinect-v2 recording (KIMORE, healthy control \texttt{NE\_ID17/Es1}; $783$
frames at $30.3\,\mathrm{fps}$), not a synthetic waveform. Its sensor-clock stamps are authentically
irregular ($C_V=0.25$, two real multi-frame dropouts), and we \emph{calibrate} the corruption
operators to reproduce them:
%
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
quantity & symbol & calibrated value \\ \hline
frame period      & $\mu_{\Delta t}$ & $33.0\,\mathrm{ms}$ ($30.3\,\mathrm{fps}$)\\
thermal jitter    & $\sigma_j$       & $0.53\,\mathrm{ms}$ \\
drop rate         & $\pi_B$          & $1.3\%$ \\
mean burst length & $1/p_{BG}$       & $5$ frames \\
reference $C_V$   & $C_V^{\mathrm{ref}}$ & $0.250$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
%
Three published, physically motivated operators act on the \emph{timestamps} (never fabricating
positions): bursty drops via a two-state Gilbert--Elliott chain; jitter
$\tilde t_k=t_k+\eta_k+d_k$ with Gaussian thermal noise and an AR(1) drift ($\alpha=0.9$),
monotonicity enforced by projection; and a Gamma inter-arrival model with an
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck-modulated mean. At the calibrated operating point the synthetic stream reproduces
the anchor to within $10.5\%$ in $C_V$ ($0.276$ vs.\ $0.250$).

\paragraph{Hash-locked fairness.}
For each corruption level and seed the pipeline emits a single set of corrupted samples
$\{(\tilde t_k,\bm{x}_k)\}$, and \emph{both} architectures consume those very bytes. Ours reads the
actual stamps ($\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{Id}$); the fixed-grid baseline first applies a \emph{best-case}
operator $\mathcal{R}\in\{\text{linear},\text{cubic},\text{forward-fill}\}$ and we report whichever is
strongest for it at every level. We compute a SHA-256 digest at each pathway's input gate and assert
byte-identity across the sweep, so no objection that the loader fed the two pathways subtly different
data is available.

\paragraph{Equal augmentation budget.}
Where a training-time robustness intervention is used (Sec.~\ref{sec:results:resampling}), it is drawn
from a \emph{single shared} corrupted stream that is byte-identical across arms (per-fold digests
asserted equal). Augmenting only the proposed model would be the sabotaged-baseline objection with
its sign reversed. Models train on drops $\sim\mathcal{U}[0,0.3]$ and are \emph{tested} to $0.7$, so
the hardest levels are genuine extrapolation rather than a memorised attack. Rotations, by contrast,
are \emph{never} applied at training time: a flat cross-viewpoint curve obtained under rotation
augmentation would prove only that we had augmented.

\paragraph{Two metrics, and why both are mandatory.}
For each method $m$ and corruption level $\pi$ we report degradation against the method's \emph{own}
clean score, and accuracy against the clinical label,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metrics}
\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{Degr}(m,\pi)=\big|s_m(\pi)-s_m(0)\big|,\\[2pt]
\mathrm{MAD}(m,\pi)=\big|s_m(\pi)-y\big| ,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
%
so no cross-method scaling can bias the comparison. Degradation alone is \emph{not} sufficient and we
refuse to report it alone: \textbf{a model that ignores its input has degradation identically zero and
would ``win'' every robustness plot ever drawn.} Accuracy-versus-level is what makes the claim mean
anything, and it is interpretable only because the pooled models provably clear the mean-predictor
floor. We therefore gate every experiment on that condition --- the gate is an assertion in the
evaluation code, not an appeal to the reader's trust --- and we draw the floor on every figure. The
continuous-flow control of \S\ref{sec:method:cde-control} is the reason this gate exists: it is exactly
a model with a perfect certificate, a perfectly flat curve, and no signal.

The converse failure is equally real and is why degradation is reported \emph{alongside} accuracy
rather than in place of it. A rotation-augmented baseline has a flat cross-viewpoint \emph{accuracy}
curve (Sec.~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint}) yet a per-sequence degradation of $3.03$ MAD: an individual
patient's score moves by three points of fifty when the camera is moved, and the errors merely cancel
in the aggregate. Aggregate accuracy cannot see this; a clinical instrument is required to give the
same patient the same score from a different room. Neither metric is sufficient alone, in either
direction.

\paragraph{Training is nondeterministic; every number is a mean over three seeds.}
Our training is not bit-reproducible even at a fixed seed. The cuDNN GRU backward pass and the
$e3nn$ encoder's scatter-add (\texttt{index\_add\_}) both accumulate with non-deterministic atomics, so
identical configurations diverge run to run. We measured the resulting spread rather than assuming it
away: repeated fits of the same (seed, fold) differ by $\approx\!0.33$~MAD --- \textbf{larger than
every clean-accuracy gap between the leading models in this paper}. Reporting a single run would
therefore have manufactured a result. Every accuracy figure below is consequently a mean $\pm$ standard
deviation over \emph{three} independent training seeds ($\times$ five subject-disjoint folds), and no
accuracy difference smaller than this floor is interpreted as a difference. Checkpoints are keyed by
(variant, seed, fold); an earlier scheme omitted the variant and an ablation silently overwrote the
headline model. We note that the structural claims are \emph{exempt} from this caution by
construction: Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} and gates E1--E8 hold for arbitrary weights, so the
degradation columns are weight-independent and no amount of seed noise can move them.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
Parameter counts are measured, not quoted: the fixed-grid transformer baseline carries
$4{,}914{,}609$ parameters ($21.0$~MB), the proposed model $565{,}881$ ($2.3$~MB) --- $\mathbf{8.7\times}$
fewer parameters and a $\mathbf{9.2\times}$ smaller memory footprint --- rising to $660{,}345$
($7.4\times$ fewer) once the parity-odd channels of \eqref{eq:cut} are admitted. The hand-crafted
invariant control is smaller still ($\sim\!0.21$M). The efficiency claim is thus retained without
argument: the structural guarantees are obtained at a fraction of the baseline's capacity, not by
spending more of it.

\paragraph{Two disclosures about the input.}
First, the loader caps every recording at $150$ frames by \emph{uniform index subsample} while
preserving its true duration. A median $22.8$~s KIMORE recording therefore reaches every model at
$\approx\!6.6$~Hz (Nyquist $\approx\!3.3$~Hz), \emph{not} the sensor's $30$~Hz. This is a deliberate
cost control, it is applied identically to every arm, and it is load-bearing for the interpretation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results:resampling}: the input is already band-limited well below any frequency at which
the resampling operator $\mathcal{R}$ is lossy. Second, all frequency-domain analyses in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results:resampling} are therefore run on the \emph{unsubsampled} $30$~Hz signal, since at
$6.6$~Hz the band under test does not exist.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We report five findings, two of them negative. Section~\ref{sec:results:protocol} establishes what
the benchmark can and cannot measure, and is a precondition for reading anything that follows.
Section~\ref{sec:results:accuracy} shows that on clean, frontal data our model, a hand-crafted
invariant control, and the baseline are statistically indistinguishable --- so clean accuracy is
not where the case is made. Section~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint} presents the structural result:
exact viewpoint invariance, held as a theorem rather than learned; the capacity tax the baseline
pays to approximate it by augmentation; and the sense in which a flat accuracy curve is
\emph{not} invariance. Section~\ref{sec:results:resampling} reports a negative result --- on KIMORE
the resampling operator that fixed-grid baselines must apply is not a lossy tax but an effective
denoiser --- and then derives the operating condition under which that null was predictable in
advance, verifying both halves of it. Section~\ref{sec:results:nodefail} answers the question the
accuracy tie leaves open: what the steerable encoder buys, if not accuracy. The answer is survival
of a broken sensor, and it is measured rather than argued.

All models are trained pooled over the five exercises with subject-disjoint $5$-fold
cross-validation, the reported epoch selected on a held-out validation fold. Scores are in the
KIMORE clinical range and MAD is reported in those units. \emph{Floor} denotes the per-exercise
mean predictor fit on the training split: a model that does not beat it has not learned
subject-level skill, whatever its MAD. \textbf{Nondeterminism.} Training is not bit-reproducible:
the cuDNN GRU backward pass and the $e3nn$ encoder's scatter-add both use nondeterministic atomics,
and the resulting run-to-run spread ($\approx\!0.33$~MAD) exceeds the accuracy gaps between the
leading models. We therefore report every accuracy metric as mean\,$\pm$\,std over three independent
seeds, and treat any gap smaller than that spread as a tie rather than a result. The structural
claim of Section~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint} is exempt from this caution by construction: it is a
property of the architecture, not of any particular trained weights.

\subsection{What the Benchmark Measures: A Protocol Audit}
\label{sec:results:protocol}

Published MADs on KIMORE Exercise~1 are not comparable to subject-disjoint numbers, but not for
the reason one first assumes. We audited the mechanism directly and record the negative result to
prevent its re-derivation: \textbf{it is not subject leakage}. In Exercise~1 each subject
contributes exactly one recording, so a random \texttt{train\_test\_split} over samples is
\emph{already} subject-disjoint. There is no subject to leak, and the split is not at fault.

The actual mechanism is \textbf{optimistic epoch selection}. The reference protocol evaluates on
the test set at every epoch and reports the minimum, with no validation split; a secondary
train$+$test \texttt{StandardScaler} fit compounds it. Holding model, data and split fixed and
changing only epoch selection isolates the bias exactly (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}).

Two things follow, and the second matters more than the first.

\textbf{(i) The inflation is real but slice-dependent.} On Exercise~1 optimistic selection is worth
$+1.111$~MAD ($16.8\%$); pooled over five exercises it shrinks to $+0.261$~MAD ($4.3\%$). Any
comparison that mixes the two slices is meaningless, so we fix the pooled protocol for all
subsequent experiments.

\textbf{(ii) On Exercise~1, the honest number is indistinguishable from the mean predictor.} The
paired subject-cluster bootstrap gives $\Delta=-0.081$ with a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.803,+0.625]$, which
straddles zero. The baseline, measured honestly, does not beat a model that ignores its input
entirely. Subject-level score regression on that slice therefore has \emph{no signal}, and the MAD
improvements reported on it are not measuring assessment skill --- a stronger and more troubling
conclusion than the selection bias. Pooling the five exercises restores signal
($\Delta=-2.228$, CI $[-3.045,-1.429]$), and this is precisely why we pool: it is the only regime
in which a degradation curve is interpretable at all.

This last point is a load-bearing methodological constraint, not a caveat. A model that ignores its
input has \emph{zero} degradation under any corruption and would ``win'' every robustness plot ever
drawn. We therefore gate every experiment below on the model provably clearing the floor, report
accuracy against the clinical label alongside degradation, and draw the floor on every figure.

\subsection{Clean-Data Accuracy Is a Tie --- and That Is the Point}
\label{sec:results:accuracy}

On clean, frontal data the proposed model does not beat the baseline, and we do not claim it does
(Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}). The steerable $e3nn$ model (EGRU, $6.62\pm0.08$), a control that feeds
\emph{hand-crafted} $SO(3)$ invariants --- bone lengths, joint radii, key inter-joint distances,
per-joint speeds --- into a plain GRU (InvariantGRU, $6.51\pm0.07$), and the point-cloud transformer
baseline (PCT, $6.47\pm0.20$) are mutually indistinguishable: every pairwise gap is smaller than the
$0.33$~MAD seed spread, and the EGRU\,$-$\,InvariantGRU difference ($+0.11$) changes sign across
seeds.

Two consequences shape how the rest of the section must be read. First, \textbf{the win, when it
comes in Section~\ref{sec:results:viewpoint}, is not a win of $e3nn$ machinery over a weaker
architecture.} A GRU on six lines of hand-crafted invariant features is exactly as accurate and ---
as we show next --- exactly as viewpoint-invariant. The advantage belongs to \emph{building
invariance in}, by whatever means, not to steerable tensor products specifically; we therefore
present InvariantGRU as a co-result rather than bury it as an ablation. What then justifies the
steerable machinery at all? Not accuracy, and we decline to argue the point rhetorically: the
justification is deferred to Section~\ref{sec:results:nodefail}, where it is \emph{measured}, and
where the hand-crafted arm --- which names the joints it depends on --- turns out to be destroyed by
a single failed tracking node that the graph-structured encoder absorbs. Second, this tie is exactly
why clean MAD cannot carry the paper --- and, per Section~\ref{sec:results:protocol}, on the
single-exercise slice it measures nothing anyway. The case rests on the structural axes.

\textbf{Chirality is free.} Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} also reports both arms with the parity-odd
channels of \eqref{eq:cut} admitted, i.e.\ with the guaranteed invariance group narrowed from $O(3)$
to $SO(3)$. The change is invisible in accuracy --- $+0.11$~MAD for EGRU, $-0.20$ for InvariantGRU,
both inside the $0.33$ seed floor and of opposite sign --- which is the honest finding: on a corpus of
healthy-form exercises, the parity-odd subspace carries no score-relevant signal. It costs $16.7\%$
more parameters and nothing else, and in exchange the architecture no longer \emph{assumes} that
handedness is irrelevant. We adopt the chiral variant as the proposed model on that basis, not on an
accuracy basis.

\subsection{Viewpoint Invariance Is a Theorem, Not a Fit}
\label{sec:results:viewpoint}

We rotate the \emph{test} skeletons about the gravity axis through a camera azimuth of
$0^\circ\!\to\!180^\circ$. Crucially, rotations are \textbf{never} applied at training time: had we
augmented, a flat curve would demonstrate only that we had augmented.

Before measuring, we certify. The architecture satisfies an $SO(3)$-intertwining constraint whose
violation we audit numerically (E1--E8, \texttt{src/certify\_egru.py}): equivariance violation is
$\sim\!10^{-16}$ --- machine roundoff --- and, decisively, its log--log slope against rotation angle
is $+0.160$, i.e.\ \emph{flat}. A modelling violation grows with angle; roundoff does not. The
fp32/fp64 violation ratio is $8.0\times10^{8}$, confirming the residual is floating-point noise
rather than a broken symmetry. Read-out invariance and translation invariance hold to
$2.6\times10^{-16}$, and E8 re-certifies read-out invariance at $2.9\times10^{-16}$ \emph{after} the
parity-odd channels are admitted: restoring chirality forfeits none of the guarantee.

The empirical consequence is exact, and it holds for \emph{both} invariant models across all three
seeds and both parity variants (Table~\ref{tab:viewpoint}). The EGRU and the hand-crafted
InvariantGRU scores are unchanged at every azimuth: degradation is $0.00$ to machine precision
(worst case $9.1\times10^{-6}$ MAD for EGRU in fp32, exactly $0$ for InvariantGRU), with zero seed
variance --- as it must be, since invariance here is a closed-form property of the read-out, not a
fitted one. The point-cloud baseline, which must learn its invariance from frontal training data,
decays monotonically and \textbf{crosses the mean-predictor floor between $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$}:
viewed from an oblique camera it becomes worse than predicting the exercise mean, reaching
$10.4$--$10.8$~MAD by $120^\circ$--$180^\circ$.

\paragraph{The capacity tax of learned invariance.} The baseline is not defenceless: rotation
invariance can be \emph{bought} with the standard remedy, training-time azimuth augmentation, and we
grant it exactly that in a fourth arm (PCT\,+\,rot). Doing so does flatten its viewpoint response ---
it no longer collapses with angle, holding near $7.6$~MAD across the sweep. But the price is a
\textbf{flat $+1.0$~MAD penalty} relative to un-augmented PCT (from $6.47$ to $7.47\pm0.23$), which
parks it \emph{at} the mean-predictor floor at every angle. Forcing a fixed-frame model to learn a
symmetry that a suitable architecture is simply \emph{born with} costs, at this sample size, roughly
the whole margin above the floor.

\paragraph{A flat accuracy curve is not invariance.} This is the distinction on which the section
turns, and aggregate MAD cannot express it. PCT\,+\,rot is flat in \emph{accuracy}, but its
\emph{degradation} --- the movement of a given sequence's own score --- reaches $\mathbf{3.03}$~MAD.
Its flatness is statistical, not structural: individual patients' scores swing by up to three points
of fifty as the camera moves, and the errors merely cancel in the mean. Ours move by
$9\times10^{-6}$. A screening instrument is required to return the \emph{same patient the same score
from a different room}, and that requirement is a statement about each prediction, not about a
population average. Augmentation buys the average; only the architecture buys the guarantee. This is
the precise sense in which the invariance is a theorem rather than a fit, and it is why we report
degradation and accuracy side by side rather than either alone.

\paragraph{The guarantee is not an artifact of synthetic rotation.} A skeptical reader may object
that rotating test skeletons about the gravity axis is an \emph{engineered} stressor. The
identical encoder answers the same objection on a \emph{real} cross-camera benchmark, and we wire it
in here rather than defer it. On NTU~RGB+D~$60$ Cross-View the train and test recordings originate
from physically distinct cameras --- setups $2$ and $3$ record the training split, and the
$45^\circ$-displaced setup~$1$ records a test split the model never sees at any angle during
training (the test stream is never rotation-augmented). Trained \textbf{without any rotation
augmentation}, our equivariant encoder transfers to the held-out camera at $82.98\%$ top-$1$, and the
same E1--E8 certificate covers this displacement --- carried onto a $60$-class action head rather than
a KIMORE regression head --- by inheritance from the group, not by a separate NTU-specific
measurement, which we did not run. The guarantee is not specific to
the $45^\circ$ camera displacement that defines this split, nor to azimuthal motion: the certificate
(E1--E8) quantifies over Haar-random proper rotations spanning the \emph{full} $SO(3)$ up to angle
$\pi$, and the cross-view displacement is one instance of that group. Logit invariance is therefore
exact under \emph{arbitrary} proper rotations of the input, of which the benchmark's inter-camera
geometry is a special case. We state plainly what this is and
is not. It is \textbf{not} an accuracy win, and we do not offer it as a comparison at all: our ST-GCN
runs are not recipe-matched to this one --- they differ in temporal sampling, view-normalisation,
optimiser and node-dropout augmentation --- so no delta between them would be interpretable, and we
therefore report none. $82.98\%$ also sits some five points below the published $\approx\!88\%$ for a
fully tuned ST-GCN on this split~\cite{yan2018stgcn}, under a single-clip $T{=}100$ recipe (causal
sampling, root-relative coordinates, AdamW) that we did not tune to the published leaderboard ---
entirely consistent with the clean-data tie of Section~\ref{sec:results:accuracy}, where accuracy was
never the axis. What it \emph{is} is the KIMORE result reproduced on real hardware: a model whose viewpoint
invariance must be \emph{learned} either sees the test camera's pose in its training distribution or
degrades, whereas ours carries the held-out $45^\circ$ camera \emph{for free and per prediction},
because the invariance is a property of the architecture and not of the training views. The
cross-view generalisation others buy with augmentation, we hold by construction --- and here on a
benchmark with genuinely different cameras, not a synthetic sweep.

\subsection{The Operational Envelope of Resampling: A Negative Result}
\label{sec:results:resampling}

Our second structural claim --- that consuming irregular arrival times natively beats projecting
them onto a uniform grid --- \textbf{does not hold on KIMORE}. We report this in full, because the
mechanism is instructive and because the claim is widely assumed.

We sweep Gilbert--Elliott burst drops with timestamp jitter from $0\%$ to $70\%$ at test time. Both
methods receive the byte-identical corrupted timeline (SHA-256 asserted equal at every
(fold, level, seed) cell); ours reads the actual stamps, the baseline must first apply the
resampling operator $\mathcal{R}$, again at its best operator.

\emph{A first run was uninformative and we discard it as a training artifact.} Trained on clean,
near-uniform arrivals ($\Delta t\!\le\!0.026$\,s) and then attacked with gaps up to $0.22$\,s ---
roughly $10\times$ outside its training support --- our model collapsed through the floor
($6.43\!\to\!10.2$~MAD). The recurrence consumes $\Delta t$ natively, but it had never learned what
a large gap \emph{means}. This is a deficiency of the training distribution, not of the
architecture, and to test the structural question it must be removed.

We therefore grant \textbf{both} arms an identical augmentation budget, drawn from a single shared
corrupted training stream that is byte-identical across arms (per-fold hash asserted equal).
Augmenting only our method would be the ``sabotaged baseline'' objection with its sign reversed.
Models are trained on drops $\sim\mathcal{U}[0,0.3]$ but \emph{tested} to $0.7$, so the two hardest
levels are genuine extrapolation rather than a memorised attack.

\subsubsection{The Null Is Predicted, Not Merely Observed}
\label{sec:results:bandwidth}

A negative result is only worth reporting if it is explained. We state the operating condition
exactly, and then verify both of its halves.

A grid-free model and a resampled model see the \emph{same surviving samples}; irregularity hands
the former no extra data. They differ in exactly two respects: the grid-free model never fabricates
values by interpolation, and it retains the true arrival times. It can therefore only win where
those two things matter:
%
\begin{quote}
\emph{A grid-free model beats a resampled one if and only if the task depends on spectral content
that $\mathcal{R}$ destroys.}
\end{quote}

\textbf{(a) KIMORE does not satisfy the condition.} A model-free spectral census of the corpus at
its native $30.3$~Hz settles this without reference to any checkpoint. Of the positional motion
energy, $86.3\%$ lies below $0.5$~Hz and $97.7\%$ below $2$~Hz. The baseline's uniform grid ($100$
frames over a median $22.8$~s recording) has a Nyquist corner of $2.19$~Hz, so $\mathcal{R}$ destroys
just $\mathbf{2.2\%}$ of the positional energy. It destroys $70\%$ of the \emph{velocity} band --- but
velocity is a first difference, i.e.\ a high-pass filter, and that band is dominated by Kinect's
broadband joint noise rather than by movement. \textbf{$\mathcal{R}$ therefore discards mostly noise},
which is precisely why the fixed-grid baseline is flat, and indeed mildly \emph{helped}, under
corruption. The operator the fixed-grid architecture is forced to apply turns out to protect it. The
null of Table~\ref{tab:resampling} is thus a prediction of the corpus, derivable before the
experiment was run.

\textbf{(b) The mechanism nevertheless exists.} To confirm that the condition is the right one --- and
not a post-hoc rationalisation --- we place signal in the band $\mathcal{R}$ destroys and re-test. We
inject a physiologically calibrated $5$~Hz tremor (the Parkinsonian band is $4$--$6$~Hz) at a random
amplitude into the wrist and hand joints, apply the identical Gilbert--Elliott corruption, and
recover the amplitude two ways: by resampling onto the uniform grid and matched-filtering, versus by
fitting the tone directly on the actual irregular stamps (the Lomb--Scargle construction). The
grid-free estimator tracks the true amplitude at $r=\mathbf{1.000}$ at \emph{every} drop level from
$0\%$ to $70\%$, while the resampled estimator decays monotonically, $0.998\!\to\!0.785$. Irregular
samples have no single Nyquist frequency and do not alias; a uniform grid at the same \emph{average}
rate folds the tone onto itself irrecoverably. The mechanism is real and behaves exactly as the theory
requires.

\textbf{Scope.} We therefore claim no irregular-sampling advantage on KIMORE, and we do not attribute
its absence to a defect of the method. We claim a \emph{boundary}: grid-free modelling pays when the
clinically relevant dynamics lie above the resampling corner --- tremor, spasticity, clonus, or real
sensor dropout on fast motion --- and KIMORE, a corpus of slow prescribed exercises whose scores live
below $2$~Hz, sits strictly inside the region where it cannot. A note of caution about our own
pipeline reinforces this: the loader subsamples each recording to $150$ frames while preserving its
duration, so every model here in fact sees $\approx\!6.6$~Hz, band-limiting the input still further.
The regime that would test the claim is not reachable on this data, and we decline to assert the
advantage without a corpus that can test it.

\subsection{What the Steerable Encoder Buys: Sensor-Node Failure}
\label{sec:results:nodefail}

Section~\ref{sec:results:accuracy} left an obligation outstanding. If a plain GRU on hand-crafted
invariants is as accurate as the $e3nn$ encoder \emph{and} exactly as viewpoint-invariant, then the
steerable machinery is unjustified --- and no amount of appeal to elegance repairs that. We discharge
the obligation with a measurement rather than an argument.

A Kinect tracking node fails: it stops updating and reports a stale estimate. We model this
literally, freezing $k$ randomly chosen non-root joints at their first-frame position for the whole
sequence --- motion destroyed, geometry left anatomically plausible, which is the \emph{conservative}
version of the failure. All three arms receive the byte-identical corrupted sequence (SHA-256 asserted
equal), no model is retrained, and results are pooled over three seeds and five folds
(Table~\ref{tab:nodefail}).

The hand-crafted arm does not degrade under a single dead joint; it \textbf{collapses through the
mean-predictor floor} ($6.31\!\to\!9.78$~MAD at $k=1$), and reaches $18.4$ by $k=8$. The EGRU loses
$3.8$~MAD over the same range and stays below the floor to $k=4$. The mechanism is structural, and it
is the one thing the two invariant models genuinely do not share: InvariantGRU's features
\emph{name} the joints they depend on --- specific bone lengths, specific inter-joint distances,
per-joint speeds, all concatenated in index order --- so killing a joint does not attenuate a feature,
it corrupts a coordinate the recurrence has learned to trust. The EGRU pools \emph{learned messages
over the skeleton graph} by mean and max, so a dead node is diluted across $25$ joints and its
neighbours partially reconstruct it. This is invisible in every clean-accuracy column in this paper,
which is exactly why it had to be measured separately.

We record two honest qualifications. First, \textbf{the point-cloud transformer is the most
node-robust model of the three} ($+2.3$~MAD at $k=8$): attention over a point cloud can simply
down-weight an anomalous point. Robustness to node failure is not a property of equivariance, and we
do not claim it is. Second, the EGRU is not immune --- it is merely $3\times$ more graceful than the
hand-crafted alternative. The correct reading is not ``ours is most robust'' but ``\emph{among models
with an exact viewpoint guarantee}, only the graph-structured one survives a broken sensor.''

steerable encoder is measurably the more node-fail-robust of the two --- the outcome that justifies its
cost.

\subsection{Per-Family Contribution of the Invariant Cut}
\label{sec:results:ablation}

To quantify what each family of the cut \eqref{eq:cut} contributes, we zero one family at a time at
\emph{inference} on the deployed chiral checkpoints, preserving the projection dimension so the
ablation runs without retraining (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). This isolates the trained model's
\emph{dependence} on each family; it is not the marginal value of \emph{adding} a family at training,
which for the parity-odd subspace is already the $O(3)$-vs-$SO(3)$ retrain comparison of
Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}, and the two readings are reconciled below.

\textbf{Accuracy.} Three families carry it. Removing the pairwise \emph{cosines} costs $1.83$~MAD and
the learned \emph{triple products} $2.75$, with bone lengths a distant third ($0.67$); the scalar,
vector-norm and anatomical-volume families are each inside the $0.33$ seed floor ($+0.25$, $+0.16$,
$+0.19$) and are inert. The pairwise angular structure of the encoder's vector channels is where the
score lives.

\textbf{The parity-odd families split, and that reconciles the chirality null.} The two parity-odd
families behave oppositely: the \emph{learned} triple products are heavily relied upon ($+2.75$),
while the \emph{fixed anatomical} volumes are inert ($+0.19$). This is consistent with --- not
contrary to --- the finding of Section~\ref{sec:results:accuracy} that \emph{adding} chirality is
free ($+0.11$~MAD, Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}). That null is a \emph{retrain} comparison: a model
trained without the parity-odd subspace matches it, because the parity-even families substitute for it
during optimisation. The present ablation is an \emph{inference} comparison on a model trained
\emph{with} it: given learnable triple-product channels the optimiser routes signal through them, so
zeroing them at test time starves weights that expect them. The fixed anatomical volumes, carrying
only a handedness that healthy-form exercises never reward, are never co-opted --- the chirality null,
now localised to the family that embodies it. Both statements hold: the parity-odd subspace is free to
add and, once added, used.

\textbf{Robustness.} The node-failure column (worst MAD lost over $8$ dead joints, against the full
cut's $+3.76$) resolves the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:results:nodefail} to family granularity.
Removing the cosines is doubly harmful --- it inflates node-failure degradation the most ($+7.29$), so
the angular structure carries graceful degradation as well as accuracy. Conversely, the families that
most \emph{name} their joints are the brittleness locus: zeroing bone lengths ($+2.36$) or the learned
triple products ($-0.16$) \emph{flattens} the node-failure response, but at an accuracy cost that puts
the model through the floor, which is why the cut keeps them. This is the within-model echo of the
between-model result of Section~\ref{sec:results:nodefail}: features that name joints trade robustness
for the accuracy they carry.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:results:limits}

We flag five, all measured rather than anticipated.

\emph{The continuous-time variant does not work.} The Neural CDE, evaluated identically, reaches
$8.427$~MAD against a floor of $8.249$ --- it \emph{fails} the mean-predictor floor and beats it on
only $3/5$ folds. Its equivariance certificate is nonetheless exact, which is the cautionary point,
and the reason our evaluation gates on the floor: a valid structural guarantee on a model without
signal produces a perfectly flat, entirely meaningless robustness curve. We report the CDE as a
control and rest no claim on it. This is also why nondeterminism, which shifts absolute MAD by up to
$0.33$, never threatens the central result: the flat curve is a theorem about the architecture, and
it stayed exactly flat across every seed even as the accuracy anchor moved.

\emph{Clean-data accuracy is a tie, not a win.} On undistorted frontal data EGRU, InvariantGRU, and
PCT are statistically indistinguishable (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}); we make no clean-accuracy claim
and the case rests entirely on the structural axes. In particular we do \emph{not} claim the $e3nn$
machinery is necessary \emph{for accuracy}: a GRU on hand-crafted invariants matches it, and on this
corpus that is the more parameter-efficient way to obtain an exact viewpoint guarantee. Its
justification is confined to what Section~\ref{sec:results:nodefail} measures.

\emph{Augmentation is a viable alternative to architecture, and we say so.} Rotation-augmented PCT
clears the floor under both stressors (Table~\ref{tab:pareto}). Our claim against it is not that it
fails but that its invariance is statistical rather than exact ($3.03$ vs.\ $9\times10^{-6}$ MAD of
per-sequence score shift) and that it costs $+1.0$~MAD and $7.4\times$ the parameters. A reader who
regards a three-point-in-fifty viewpoint-dependent score swing as clinically acceptable should prefer
the augmented baseline, and we would not dispute it.

\emph{The irregular-sampling advantage does not hold here, and could not have.}
Section~\ref{sec:results:bandwidth} shows the resampling operator destroys only $2.2\%$ of KIMORE's
positional energy and acts as a denoiser; the mechanism is real (grid-free tremor recovery at
$r=1.000$ under $70\%$ drops) but the corpus does not exercise it. The claim is deferred to a corpus
with genuine high-frequency clinical content, not asserted on this one. We note that our own loader,
which subsamples to $150$ frames, band-limits the input further --- so this paper cannot test the
claim even in principle, and a follow-up must run at the sensor's native rate.

\emph{Chirality is restored but unrewarded.} The parity-odd channels close a genuine hole --- the cut
was silently $O(3)$-invariant and provably blind to trajectory handedness (E6) --- at no cost to the
viewpoint theorem (E8). But they buy no accuracy on KIMORE ($\pm0.2$~MAD, inside the seed floor and of
inconsistent sign), because a corpus of healthy-form prescribed exercises contains no chiral
pathology to detect. The fix is principled, not empirically vindicated, and we present it as the
former. A corpus with unilateral or rotational pathology is required to reward it.

\emph{Augmented-training floor margin is thin on one fold.} Under drop augmentation the EGRU clears
the mean-predictor floor in aggregate on all three seeds ($6.80$--$7.00$ vs.\ $8.25$--$8.38$), but
on $1$ of $15$ (seed, fold) cells it does not ($6.96$ vs.\ $6.82$). Degradation on that cell is
reported but not interpreted, per the same gate.

\section{Edge Deployment: Precision Budget and Streaming Latency}
\label{sec:deploy}

The viewpoint guarantee is a statement about real arithmetic; a device in a patient's home runs
reduced precision and must produce a score before the exercise is over. We close the paper by
measuring what the theorem costs on edge hardware, on the \emph{deployed} clinical model and on a
causal, real-time variant of the encoder. Both are deployment questions the clean-accuracy column
cannot ask.

\subsection{Equivariance Has a Precision Budget}
\label{sec:deploy:precision}

Quantisation is not an orthogonal transformation, so it does not commute with the Wigner-$D$ action:
the exact identity $s(g\cdot x)=s(x)$ acquires a numerical floor once the representation is stored in
fewer bits. We measure it on the trained pooled EGRU over $40$ real KIMORE sequences $\times\,64$
camera azimuths, constructing each arithmetic \emph{natively} (the Clebsch--Gordan tables are built
in the target format, not cast after the fact, which would report a spurious floor). We report two
quantities: the relative violation of the $160$-dimensional invariant projection --- the true
arithmetic floor, which does not saturate --- and the resulting movement of the reported $0$--$50$
score, in the MAD units a clinician reads (Table~\ref{tab:precision}). The fp32 row reproduces the
$9\times10^{-6}$~MAD residual of Table~\ref{tab:viewpoint}: that number was the fp32 point of a ladder.

The floor climbs monotonically with the mantissa, spanning fourteen decades from fp64 to int8 ---
equivariance has a measurable precision budget. Two details matter for deployment. First, fp16 is
$3.2\times$ tighter than bf16 despite equal width: bf16 trades mantissa for exponent range, and
equivariance pays in mantissa bits, so fp16 is the better half-precision target for this model.
Second, int8 (simulated per-tensor symmetric fake-quantisation with an MSE-calibrated scale, since
\texttt{e3nn} has no native int8 kernel) falls \emph{off} the float curve, and decomposing it is the
point: quantising the \emph{weights} leaves the floor at its fp32 value ($3.1\times10^{-7}$), because
the encoder is equivariant for arbitrary weights --- a quantised model is simply a different
equivariant model. The cliff to $3.4\times10^{-2}$ appears only when the \emph{activations} are
quantised, because rounding the type-$1$ (vector) channels and the rotated joints onto a fixed grid
is basis-dependent: $\mathrm{round}(\rho(g)\,v)\neq\rho(g)\,\mathrm{round}(v)$. The structural break
is an activation-quantisation phenomenon, not a weight-precision one.

Yet the \emph{clinical} consequence stays small. Even at int8, the reported score moves by
$0.05$~MAD --- two orders below the mean-predictor floor and $60\times$ smaller than the $3.03$~MAD
per-sequence swing that camera rotation inflicts on the augmented baseline (Table~\ref{tab:viewpoint}).
Int8 shatters \emph{exact} structural equivariance while leaving the deployed instrument's output
clinically stationary: the honest arithmetic number and the honest deployment number are different
numbers, and we report both.

\subsection{Causal Streaming and Time-to-First-Score}
\label{sec:deploy:streaming}

A high-accuracy skeleton classifier is often bidirectional and pools over the whole clip, so it
cannot emit a score until the last frame arrives --- it has no honest latency. Because our viewpoint
guarantee lives entirely in a per-frame spatial encoder followed by an invariant cut, it survives
conversion to a strictly causal read-out unchanged: replacing the bidirectional recurrence with a
unidirectional one and the full-sequence pool with a causal running mean touches nothing left of the
cut. We verify this on the NTU~RGB+D~$60$ Cross-View action benchmark, where high-frequency motion
actually exercises a streaming model, using the identical equivariant encoder trained for that task;
the causal head is retrained on the frozen encoder's features (Table~\ref{tab:streaming}).

The conversion costs $3.17$ points of top-$1$ accuracy ($82.98\!\to\!79.81\%$) --- the price of
never seeing the future --- and buys a genuine real-time budget: a full incremental step (encoder,
invariant projection, one recurrent update, running-mean pool, head) runs in $8.9$~ms on a single
GPU, or $112$~frames per second, comfortably inside the $33$~ms budget of a $30$~fps sensor. The
first score therefore exists $8.9$~ms after the first frame. It is not yet \emph{trustworthy} that
early: read out at each frame, the causal running-mean prediction rises from chance ($1.9\%$ at one
frame) through $45\%$ at $50$ frames to $80.6\%$ at $100$, and locks onto the eventual class after a
median of $54$ frames. We report the whole trajectory rather than a single latency, because ``time
to a \emph{correct} score'' and ``time to \emph{a} score'' are different guarantees. Throughout, the
viewpoint theorem is undamaged by construction: causal pooling replaces the bidirectional pool with a
running mean over the \emph{same} invariant cut, so the invariance proposition carries over verbatim
and no separate numerical re-certification of the streaming head is required (we report none).
Real-time causal operation and the per-prediction guarantee are not
in tension.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to beat a point-cloud transformer on rehabilitation assessment and, on the metric the field reports, we did not: on clean frontal KIMORE our model, the baseline, and a GRU on hand-crafted invariants are statistically tied, and we quantified the nondeterminism floor ($0.33$~MAD) that makes them tied rather than merely close. We regard establishing that as a contribution rather than a concession, because it forces the real question. Clean accuracy on this benchmark cannot separate architectures; on the single-exercise slice most commonly reported, it cannot even separate a trained model from a mean predictor once epoch selection is made honest.

What separates them is what happens off the clean, frontal manifold. Our read-out is $SE(3)$-invariant by construction and certified to machine roundoff --- flat in rotation angle, with an eight-order fp32/fp64 precision-scaling signature --- so moving the camera moves a patient's score by $9\times10^{-6}$~MAD, for arbitrary weights. The baseline, which must learn its invariance, degrades past the mean predictor by $45^\circ$. Augmentation genuinely rescues it in the aggregate, and we say so; what it does not rescue is the individual prediction, which still swings by $3.03$~MAD --- three points of fifty --- purely by relocating the camera. A screening instrument must return the same patient the same score from a different room. That requirement is per-prediction, and only the architecture supplies it.

We reported two nulls without softening them. Native consumption of irregular arrival times bought nothing here, and we derived the operating condition that made the null predictable in advance: the resampling operator a fixed-grid model is forced to apply destroys $2.2\%$ of KIMORE's positional energy and functions as a denoiser, because a corpus of slow prescribed exercises has essentially no energy above the resampling corner. The mechanism is nonetheless real, and we verified it by putting signal where the operator is lossy: a grid-free estimator tracks a $5$~Hz tremor at $r=1.000$ through $70\%$ frame drops while a resampled one decays to $0.785$. Grid-free modelling is a claim about tremor, spasticity, clonus and fast motion under real dropout --- not about this corpus, and we decline to assert it on a corpus that cannot test it. Similarly, closing the parity hole is principled but empirically unrewarded: healthy-form prescribed exercises contain no chiral pathology, so the pseudo-scalars buy nothing here beyond the removal of an assumption the architecture had been making silently.

The steerable encoder earns its place on one axis, and it is measured rather than argued. When a tracking node freezes, the hand-crafted invariant model --- equally accurate, equally viewpoint-exact, three times smaller --- collapses through the mean-predictor floor at a \emph{single} dead joint, because its features name the joints they depend on. The graph encoder dilutes the same failure across the skeleton. We are careful about what this does and does not say: the point-cloud transformer is in fact the most node-robust model of the three, and robustness to node failure is not a property of equivariance. The defensible claim is the Pareto one. Among models that carry an exact, per-prediction viewpoint guarantee, only the graph-structured equivariant one survives a broken sensor --- and it does so at $7.4\times$ fewer parameters than the transformer it is compared against.

Two recommendations follow for the field. First, report a mean-predictor floor and a per-sequence degradation alongside aggregate error; without the former a robustness curve is uninterpretable, and without the latter a model whose errors merely cancel is indistinguishable from one that is invariant. Second, evaluate off the frontal manifold as a matter of course --- camera relocation and sensor-node failure are not adversarial constructions but the ordinary conditions of a device deployed in a patient's home. The corpus that would reward the two capabilities we built and could not vindicate here --- high-frequency clinical content at the sensor's native rate, and unilateral or rotational pathology --- is the one we intend to build next.
