## 2. UI-PRMD as a third corpus — BLOCKED, and the reason invalidates existing code

The plan was to build the UI-PRMD correct/incorrect binary task (1000 + 1000 Kinect files on disk,
10 subjects × 10 exercises) and push it through the same harness. **The premise was wrong.**

**UI-PRMD's Kinect `Positions` files are parent-relative bone offsets, not world coordinates.**
Inspecting `m01_s01_e01_positions.txt` as (T, 22, 3):

| joint | mean (x, y, z) cm | temporal sd |
|---|---|---|
| j00 | (−2.7, 62.2, −248.6) | (0.71, 22.4, 6.97) |
| j01 | (0, 27.2, 0) | (0, 1.01, **0**) |
| j07 | (12.4, 0, 0) | (0.73, **0**, **0**) |
| j08 | (25.9, 0, 0) | (0.68, **0**, **0**) |
| j09 | (26.1, 0, 0) | (0.81, **0**, **0**) |

Only the root has genuine 3D motion. j07–j09 are a 12.4 / 25.9 / 26.1 cm collar→upper-arm→forearm
chain lying on a single axis; 19 of 66 coordinate slots have *exactly* zero variance. The pose lives
in the `Angles/` files and recovering joint positions needs forward kinematics.

**And FK is impossible for the half we need:** `Incorrect Segmented Movements/` contains
`Kinect/Positions` only — no `Angles/`, no Vicon. There is no rotation data for the incorrect
movements anywhere on disk.

Vicon *is* world coordinates (39 markers, all genuinely moving) but only for the 990 correct reps, so
it gives no binary task either.

Side benefit: the offset structure **confirms the 22-joint order empirically** (spine chain on y,
arm chains on x, leg chains on −y, toes on z), which no local documentation states. Recorded for
whenever the missing data arrives.

### Knock-on: a latent bug in paper-2 code

`src/load_uiprmd_validity.py:9` declares *"Each file: [T, 66] = 22 Kinect joints x 3 xyz (cm)"* and
`src/prepare_uiprmd.py:6` says the same. Both treat bone offsets as world coordinates. Anything built
on them fed the model a near-static skeleton plus a root trajectory.

That includes `UIPRMD_processed/`, which is **doubly unusable**: wrong geometry *and* a synthetic
target (`prepare_uiprmd.py:17`, `Score = 35 + 15·(1 − normalised L2 to the per-movement mean
trajectory)`).

**No WACV exposure** — the paper only *cites* UI-PRMD, in the Rehab-Pile sentence; there is no
UI-PRMD experiment in it. **Paper 2 may be exposed:** the V4 "null replicates on UI-PRMD" result
(best 0.53) used this loader. A null obtained on degenerate geometry is not evidence for the null.
Flagged, not yet investigated — different paper.

`src/verify_uiprmd_vicon_incorrect.py`. The gate that matters is **not** "are there zero-variance

## 4. V4 repaired by forward kinematics — the null REPLICATES

### The FK convention is documented, not guessed

`UI-PRMD Visualize.zip` (255 KB, same Wayback capture) ships the authors' `Animation.m`, the only
normative statement of the convention. Transcribed into `src/uiprmd_fk.py`:

    eulers_2_rot_matrix([gx, by, az]) = rotz(az) @ roty(by) @ rotx(gx)     # degrees

Angle column 0→X, 1→Y, 2→Z, composed Z@Y@X; a joint's angle orients its *outgoing* bones; offsets
are per-frame; +X screen-right, +Y up, +Z out of screen.

Two things in that file are worth recording. First, the authors flag their own code: *"the
visualization is correct for the upper body joints, but it is not entirely correct for all lower
body joints. The movements that look unnatural are m03, m04, and m05."* Second, reading the chain
accumulation, **the two arm chains restart from identity** (`rot_7 = E(ang_chest)`) instead of
inheriting the torso's `E(waist) @ E(spine)`; torso and legs accumulate correctly. Both variants are
implemented (`reference` = literal transcription, `corrected` = arms inherit torso) and both are run
below, so the convention is a tested assumption rather than a load-bearing guess.

### FK is validated against Vicon, not asserted

Correct-side sequences have both Kinect and Vicon, and Vicon's up-axis is z (mm), so body height is
comparable without solving marker correspondence. FK height tracks Vicon height at r = +0.78 (m01
squat) and +0.59 (m07 arm raise). On a neutral standing frame (m07 frame 0) the reconstruction is
anatomically clean: waist 80.9 → spine 111.0 → chest 132.9 → head-tip 155.9 cm, hip 80.9 → knee 46.0
→ ankle 15.8 → toes 13.1, against a Vicon top marker at 159.0 cm.

(An earlier reading of the *time-averaged* m01 squat looked broken — knee above hip — but that is
what a deep squat's mean pose is. Not a defect.)

### Degeneracy, before and after

| built with | dead coordinate slots | median per-slot sd |
|---|---|---|
| `--geometry raw` (the original V4) | **68,000 / 132,000 (51.5%)** | **0.000** |
| `--geometry fk` | **0 / 132,000** | 2.119 |

Over half of every coordinate the original V4 models saw was a literal constant.

### The result

| geometry | best model AUROC | naive kinematic | gap | Kendall W → AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw (original bug) | 0.533 | 0.647 | −0.114 | ρ=0.257, p=0.623 |
| **fk reference** | **0.578** | **0.659** | **−0.081** | ρ=0.829, **p=0.042** |
| fk corrected (sensitivity) | 0.576 | 0.659 | −0.083 | ρ=0.886, **p=0.019** |

**The null replicates.** Fixing the geometry lifts every model (ST-GCN +0.098, Exp E +0.069) but the
best KIMORE-trained scorer still loses to a naive kinematic baseline by 0.08 AUROC. The V4 claim was
not an artifact of the broken loader, and the two FK variants agree to 0.002 — the convention quirk
does not drive it.

Two things do change, and both should be reported:

1. **Model ranking under the bug was meaningless.** TCN was the apparent best (0.533) and is now
   second-worst (0.466, −0.067 — the only model to drop). Any statement about *which* architecture
   transfers best must be withdrawn.
2. **Hypothesis (b) strengthens from null to significant.** Kendall W → AUROC was ρ=0.257, p=0.623
   (i.e. no evidence) under the bug; it is ρ=0.83–0.89, p=0.019–0.042 under real geometry. The
   reliability→validity association the paper argues for is *better* supported after the repair.
   Caveat: n=6 models, so p≈0.04 is fragile — one rank flip could undo it. Report it as suggestive
   with the n stated, not as an established effect.

### A specific threat to a headline mechanistic claim

The paper's central mechanism is the sensor-identity probe: 3-way balanced accuracy 1.00 vs 0.33
chance, argued to show encoders represent sensor identity rather than movement quality. It
pre-empts the obvious confound — "not caused by joint zero-padding: zeroing the padded and
duplicated joints $\{7,11,22,23,24\}$". That control is insufficient:

| corpus, as the pipeline saw it | constant-coordinate fraction |
|---|---|
| UI-PRMD raw (paper) | **0.573** |
| UI-PRMD fk | 0.120 (= 3/25 padded joints exactly) |
| REHAB246 | 0.000 |

Zeroing padded joints removes 0.120 of that; the remaining ~0.45 is bone-offset constants and
survives the control. UI-PRMD is therefore separable from the other corpora by a single scalar —
"what fraction of your coordinates never move" — so a perfect probe involving UI-PRMD is not
evidence of learned sensor entanglement.

Scope, stated precisely: the paper also reports **pairwise KIMORE vs REHAB246 = 1.00**, which does
not involve UI-PRMD and is untouched by this bug. The architecture-independence claim (probe perfect
on ST-GCN as well as TCN) is likewise unaffected as far as that pair goes. What needs rerunning is
every UI-PRMD-involving number.

**Not fixed here, and not fixable by editing text:** the UI-PRMD half of manuscript.tex needs
recomputing from the FK cache, which means re-running the SSL zero-shot pipeline, not just swapping
numbers. Flagged for decision — it is a substantially larger job than the V4 rerun and touches a
different paper.

### A process rule worth keeping: every probe needs a must-be-zero control

Three separate harness bugs were caught this session, and **all three were caught the same way** —
a quantity that had to read zero, or had to reproduce a published number, stopped doing so:

1. The naive shared-joints mask reused the *probe's* joint list `{7,11,22,23,24}`. Joints 7 and 11
   are REHAB24-6's duplicated permutation targets but genuine UI-PRMD joints, so masking them
   deleted real signal. Caught because the raw-geometry rerun stopped reproducing the published
   "identical at 0.538".
2. The `Rel.-joint` and `Per-seq` robustness rows were fed raw coordinates although their models
   were *trained* on transformed inputs — scoring a model on a distribution it never saw. Caught
   because raw no longer reproduced the published 0.534 ± 0.027.
3. The canonicalizer noise probe's first "better sign rule" used the summed projection, which is
   **identically zero for mean-centred data** — its sign was pure numerical noise. Caught because
   the clean-rotation control read 180° where equivariance requires 0.

None of these would have announced itself. Each produced plausible numbers that were simply wrong,
and in two cases those numbers would have gone into a paper. The rule this justifies:

> **Every probe ships with a quantity whose correct value is known in advance — zero, or a
> previously published number — and that quantity is checked before any new result is read.**

The cost is one extra column; the return this session was three prevented ship-bugs. Note also what
makes a control *work*: it must be insensitive to the effect under test. REHAB24-6 was the right
control for the UI-PRMD geometry bug precisely because the bug could not reach it; the clean-rotation
residual was the right control for the canonicalizer because exact equivariance makes it zero by
theory, independent of the noise regime being probed.
