# Response to Reviewers — Round 4 (Major Revision)

We thank the reviewer for pushing the statistical rigor of the negative result further.
Every round-4 concern is addressed with analysis on the **already-trained** models, plus
one lightweight added training run (SWAD, a single KIMORE-supervised model) to answer the
DA/DG ask, and the central claim is reframed to what the subject-level statistics actually
support. Line references are to the revised `manuscript.tex`; raw numbers are in
`outputs/reviewer_round4/`.

## Headline change
The reviewer correctly noted that each target corpus has only **ten subjects**, so
repetition-level intervals overstate power, and that "the naive baseline is unbeaten" was
over-claimed. We re-ran every condition with a **subject-cluster bootstrap** (2,000
resamples, orientation fixed once on the full sample to avoid the upward bias of
per-resample `max(AUROC, 1−AUROC)`). The honest result: **no learned model is
distinguishable from chance on REHAB246**, only trivial effects clear chance on UI-PRMD,
and the naive baseline's advantage over the learned models is **largely not significant**.
We therefore softened "definitive" → "robust" and reframed the thesis throughout: *no
method — learned or naive — achieves subject-level-meaningful cross-sensor discrimination.*

### Headline outcome of the new experiments
Across **every** architecture (TCN, ST-GCN), input representation (coordinates,
relative-joint-vectors), and domain-adaptation method (CORAL, AdaBN, DANN) we tried,
zero-shot cross-sensor AUROC stays at chance and below the naive kinematic baseline,
and the sensor-identity signal persists (perfect probe on both TCN and ST-GCN). The
negative result is therefore **not** an artifact of backbone, coordinate normalization,
joint padding, or weak adaptation — it is robust. Raw numbers: `outputs/reviewer_round3/*.json`;
reproduced by `src/run_reviewer_round3_heavy.py`.
