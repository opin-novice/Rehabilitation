## 1. The Kuang units claim — CLOSED, and the original claim was **wrong**

**The open risk (from 07-27).** `paper_wacv.tex:134` argued Kuang et al.'s MAD 0.10–0.16 was
implausibly low "even after rescaling", assuming a linear 0–100 → 0–50 conversion. Rafat's numbers
had turned out to be z-scored, not min-max. If Kuang's were too, the arithmetic failed the same way —
and the sentence is load-bearing for the "published numbers are protocol artifacts" argument.

**How it was resolved without their code.** Every method in this lineage publishes MAD *and* MAPE for
the same run, with `MAPE = 100·mean(|y−ŷ|/|y|)`. So `100·MAD/MAPE` recovers the magnitude of whatever
array was handed to the metric. Raw clinical scores predict ~35–41; z-scored targets predict
mean|z| ≈ 0.8. Those two hypotheses are 45× apart — far outside any transcription noise.

**Result.** All 40 published cells (8 methods × 5 exercises, Du 2021 → Kuang 2026, plus the
point-cloud transformer's own table) land in the score band.

| reading | predicts `100·MAD/MAPE` | observed |
|---|---|---|
| raw 0–50 clinical score | 35.0–40.7 (true per-exercise means) | **38.1 median, 10.6% median rel. err** |
| standardised (z-scored) | ≈ 0.8 | excluded by **≥70×** |

The published KIMORE numbers are in **0–50 clinical-score units — the same axis as our 6.3–6.7**.
There is no 0–100 scale and no rescaling step. Kuang et al.'s 0.10–0.16 sits **39–67× below** ours,
directly.

- Artifact: `src/published_units_audit.py` → `outputs/published_units_audit.json`. Three gates
  (score band / standardised excluded ≥20× / tracks true means ≤25%), all PASS.
- `paper_wacv.tex:134` rewritten; the arithmetic moved to supplement §S7. Main paper still 8pp + refs;
  supplement 3pp. Both compile clean, no undefined refs, no overfull boxes.

**Knock-on to the reference paper.** Their published 0.185 is score units too. So E8's
"0.483 standardised vs a claimed 0.185, short by 2.6×" was wrong: the correct comparison is 4.09 vs
0.185 — their released code at their own budget under their own test-selection lands **22× above**
their published figure. `HISTORY.md` E8 amended, open risk #4 struck, new §E10 added.

### 1b. …and the causal claim was cut (same day)

Settling the units is **not** settling the cause. The first write-up blurred them, and Opin caught it.

We never ran Kuang's pipeline; their preprocessed data isn't public; we measured no leakage in it.
Their sample-level split is a *mechanism*, not a measurement — and the only time this hypothesis was
actually tested on KIMORE it **failed** (77 sequences ≈ one per subject → a random split is already
subject-disjoint; that is exactly why the same charge against the PCT paper was withdrawn).

The paper was also self-contradictory on this point:

| line | text |
|---|---|
| :145 | "Kuang et al.'s split is itself an instance of the leakage `tab:protocol3seed` exists to measure" |
| :250 | "This is **not** subject leakage (each subject contributes one recording on that slice…)" |

**Resolution — full retreat.** The paragraph now claims only that the protocols are incomparable,
which their own "8:1:1 stratified sampling" sentence establishes and which fully justifies keeping
them out of Table 1. No causal diagnosis, no implication of misconduct. Recompiled clean, still 8pp.

Two related overclaims also rejected: "their numbers are cheating" (nothing supports it; sample-level
splitting is an unexamined convention, not fraud) and "our 6.4 is the true realistic performance"
(Table 2 says Δ = −0.44 clearing zero in 1/3 seeds — we cannot call it true performance in one
paragraph and barely-separable-from-the-mean in another).

**Honest note (kept visible).** Kuang's *released* repo (`BryceLoski21/D2STA`) standardises targets
with a `StandardScaler` and never inverse-transforms before `mad()` — which would imply z-units and
contradict the above. But that repo is visibly ablation leftovers (hard-coded `ex3`, a wandb run named
`origin_model_ex3_seed3407`) and is not what produced Table 3. The claim rests on the **published
tables**, which are self-consistent, public and checkable by any reviewer — not on repo forensics.
If a reviewer challenges the units, the MAD/MAPE identity is the answer.

## 2. Reference reproduction, multi-seed — RUNNING

**The open risk (from 07-27).** The reproduction was single-seed (145, theirs). Enough to establish
that the selection effect exists and is large; not enough to say how much of the specific 4.09 is seed
luck.

**RUNNING:** seeds 146 and 147, 2000 epochs each, their code unmodified, seeds passed through their
own `--seed` flag — `scripts/run_reference_protocol_seeds.sh` (~3.5 h/seed; seed 145 took 12,404 s).

`outputs/reference_repro_seeds.json`. Parses their per-epoch curve and reports test-selected /
late-median / final / selection effect per seed and pooled, converting standardised→score units with

test-selected value against their own `eval.py`: **max abs err 0.0003 MAD** on seed 145.

contiguous epoch indices too, and folding one in would report a truncated curve's minimum as if it
were the 2000-epoch minimum. (First version had exactly that bug; caught because seed 146 at epoch
